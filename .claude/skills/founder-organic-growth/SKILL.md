# Founder organic growth (2026)

You are helping a founder grow with time instead of money. Organic channels compound, but slowly and unevenly; your job is to pick 1–2 channels that fit the product and the founder, set weekly quotas, and kill vanity work. Benchmarks and sources live in `docs/research/marketing-and-ads.md`. Use ranges; never invent statistics.

## Step 1: Gather inputs (one message, only what is missing)

1. Product, ICP (who pays, where they hang out online), B2C or B2B, price.
2. Founder assets: network size and relevance, follower counts, email list, writing/video comfort, communities they belong to.
3. Stage: idea, waitlist, live with users, paying customers. Current traffic and conversion if known.
4. Hours per week available for marketing.
5. What they already tried and what happened.

## Step 2: Set realistic expectations up front

Say these plainly:
- Distribution is the bottleneck: 14,000+ new subscription apps launch per month and the median app makes ~$72/month a year after launch (RevenueCat 2026).
- Friends and personal network give first users and feedback, rarely revenue. Unless friends are the ICP, expect a handful of trials and 0–3 paying customers from a typical network.
- Launch days (Product Hunt, Hacker News, BetaList) produce a one-day spike: ~60% of traffic in 24 hours, low paid conversion (one public case: ~400 signups → 1 paying customer).
- Typical K-factor is 0.1–0.25; good referral programs reach 0.4–0.7; sustained K > 1 is rare. Even K = 0.2 adds ~25% on top of other acquisition.
- SEO is a 6–24 month bet; AI Overviews cut top-result CTR roughly in half on informational queries.

## Step 3: Pick channels by fit

| Situation | Primary channel | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| B2B, founder has domain credibility | Founder-led LinkedIn/X content + direct conversations | Niche newsletter, communities |
| B2B, buyers use a platform with a marketplace | Integration listing (Shopify, Slack, HubSpot, Zapier, Chrome, MCP/GPT directories) | Comparison/alternative pages |
| B2B, people search for the problem | Bottom-funnel SEO + GEO pages | Reddit/community answers |
| B2C app, visual or demoable | Short-form video (TikTok/Reels/Shorts), founder or creators | App Store optimization |
| B2C, passionate niche | Subreddits, Discords, niche creators | Referral loop |
| Selling to indie builders/marketers | Build in public on X | Product Hunt, Indie Hackers |
Choose one primary channel for 8–12 weeks before adding another.

## Step 4: Launch to the network (week 1)

1. List 50–150 contacts; tag each as ICP, connector (knows ICP), or supporter.
2. Send personal 1:1 messages, not a mass blast. Ask ICP contacts for a 15-minute call or to try it; ask connectors for one specific intro; ask supporters for feedback or a share.
3. Offer founding-customer pricing to ICP contacts to test willingness to pay; track who pays separately from who signs up.
4. Turn every conversation into an insight log entry and, with permission, a testimonial.

## Step 5: Build in public (if the audience matches the buyer)

- Post 3–5 times per week: real numbers (MRR, churn, experiments), lessons, behind-the-scenes, customer stories. Reply to 10–20 relevant accounts daily; replies grow small accounts faster than posts.
- On LinkedIn, keep links out of the post body (external-link posts get roughly 20–50% less reach in third-party studies); post from the personal profile, not the company page.
- Funnel attention into an owned channel (email list or waitlist). Measure signups per post, not likes.
- Warn the founder when the audience is other founders but the customer is not: build in public then gives morale and peers, not revenue.

## Step 6: Content, SEO and GEO

1. Start bottom of funnel: "[competitor] alternative", "[tool A] vs [tool B]", "best [category] for [niche]", "[job] template/calculator", integration pages, honest pricing page. These convert; top-of-funnel explainers mostly get answered by AI Overviews now.
2. Use AI to draft, but add what models can't: original data, screenshots, real workflows, opinions, customer quotes. Thin AI-written volume is the 2016 content-farm playbook and fails in 2026.
3. GEO/AEO: LLMs cite third-party pages. Get the product mentioned in Reddit threads, "best X" listicles, review sites (G2, Capterra, Product Hunt), YouTube reviews and comparison posts; keep clear, crawlable docs, FAQs and pricing. Check monthly what ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Mode answer for the top 10 buyer prompts and track whether the product is named.
4. Expect long-tail pages to start pulling traffic in 3–6 months and competitive terms in 12–24 months. AI-referred visitors are a small share (~0.3–1%) but convert well.
5. Cadence: 1–2 strong pages per week beats daily filler. Refresh winners quarterly.

## Step 7: Communities (Reddit, Discord, Slack, forums)

- Join where the ICP already asks questions. Spend 2–4 weeks answering helpfully before mentioning the product; follow each community's self-promotion rules exactly.
- Answer with substance; mention the product only when it directly solves the question, and disclose you built it.
- Save recurring questions as content ideas and as GEO targets (Reddit threads are heavily cited by Google and LLMs).

## Step 8: Launch platforms

- Product Hunt: launch only with a working product, a warm list to notify, and a prepared maker comment; aim for feedback, backlinks and a list bump, not revenue. Engagement (comments, maker replies) matters more than raw upvotes.
- Repeat smaller launches: each major feature is a new launch moment (changelog, email, social, communities).

## Step 9: Referral loops and UGC programs

- Referral: two-sided reward tied to product value (extra usage, credits, a free month), prompted right after a success moment, with a shareable artifact (report, result, link) where possible. Measure invites per active user × invite conversion = K.
- Product-led loops beat referral bribes: outputs that carry the brand (shared docs, embeds, "made with" badges, public pages).
- UGC/creator program (B2C): seed 10–30 micro-creators with free access and a brief; pay $150–$300 per video for paid-usage rights only after a post performs organically; pay 20–30% of the fee per 30 days for whitelisting/Spark ads. Hand winners to the `founder-paid-ads` skill.
- AI-generated creator videos are fine for testing hooks; disclose where platforms require it and never fake testimonials or reviews.

## Step 10: Deliver the plan

Produce: (1) chosen primary + secondary channel with the reason; (2) a 12-week calendar with weekly quotas (posts, pages, community answers, conversations, creator outreach); (3) the metrics to track weekly: signups by source, activation, paid conversion by source, email list growth, AI-answer mentions; (4) a stop rule: if a channel shows no leading-indicator improvement after 8 weeks of consistent effort, switch. End with the single action for today.

## Guardrails

- Call out obsolete advice: blogging daily for traffic, Product Hunt as the growth plan, invite walls for virality, mass cold email (Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender rules since 2024), link-dumping on LinkedIn/X, keyword-stuffed AI content.
- Never suggest astroturfing, fake reviews, vote rings, sock-puppet Reddit accounts or buying followers; they get accounts banned and destroy trust.
- If the user is in the simulator game, map advice to its mechanics but keep the reasoning identical to real life.
