# Investor relations and governance (2026)

You are helping a real founder communicate with people who already own part of the company. Optimize for trust over
the long run, not for looking good this month. Never fabricate metrics. Use only numbers the founder gives you, and
mark any estimate. Background and sources are in `docs/research/world-ops-support-life.md` §4 and
`public/data/world.js` (`INVESTOR_RELATIONS`). Fundraising itself is covered by the founder-fundraising skill.

## Step 1: Establish the investor base and obligations

Ask for: the instruments (SAFEs, convertible notes, priced round), whether any investor has a board seat or information
rights, the number of investors and other recipients (angels, advisors, friends), the stage, and the next planned raise.

Explain:
- SAFE holders usually have no contractual information rights and no board seat. Updates are a norm, not a duty.
- Priced rounds usually give "major investors" information rights (annual financial statements, often quarterly
  statements and a budget) and create a board. The typical seed board has 3 seats. Check the actual documents.
- Some angel groups negotiate information rights or an observer seat in side letters.

## Step 2: Pick the cadence

- Monthly from pre-seed to Series A and quarterly after Series B is the common norm (vendor guidance, consistent with
  practitioner advice). Pick one and never skip. Silence is read as bad news.
- Time budget: 1–3 hours a month, or 30–60 minutes with a template and live metrics.
- The claim that updating founders are "3× more likely to raise follow-on" has no published method. Do not cite it.
  The real reason to update is that insiders lead bridges, make intros and give references.

## Step 3: Write the update

Use this structure and keep it to one screen plus a metrics table:
1. **Subject:** company, month, and the one-line headline (e.g. "Acme, Sept: MRR $18.2k (+9%), 14 months runway").
2. **TL;DR:** 2–3 sentences. Good news and bad news both.
3. **KPIs table** with definitions that never change: MRR or revenue, growth %, new/churned customers, gross and net
   burn, cash, runway in months, headcount, and 1–2 product metrics (activation, retention). Show prior month and the
   plan.
4. **Highlights:** 3–5 bullets with specifics (customer names only if allowed).
5. **Lowlights:** 1–3 honest bullets, each with what you learned and what you are changing.
6. **Asks:** up to 3–4 specific, easy-to-act-on requests, such as "Intro to the head of ops at a 50–200 person 3PL" or
   "Anyone who has priced usage-based AI credits: 20 minutes?". Avoid "let me know if you can help".
7. **Thanks:** name people who helped last month. This rewards help.

Review drafts against these rules: numbers match prior updates, definitions are stable, no vanity metrics without the
denominator, bad news appears near the top and is not buried, and asks are specific.

## Step 4: Deliver bad news

- Tell investors early, in the next update or sooner by email if it is material (a key customer churned, runway under 6
  months, a co-founder left, a pivot).
- Use this format: what happened, why, what you are doing, what you need, and when they will hear next.
- If runway is under ~9 months, raise the bridge question with the largest holders individually *before* the update goes
  out. Surprise bad news during a raise damages trust far more than early bad news.

## Step 5: Run the board (after a priced round)

- Cadence is every 6–8 weeks at seed or Series A or quarterly. Meetings run 1.5–3 hours. Send the deck 48–72 hours ahead.
- Prep time [estimate]: 6–12 hours for the first meeting, then 3–6 hours once metrics are automated.
- Agenda: (1) CEO summary and the 1–2 decisions needed, (2) metrics vs plan, (3) a deep dive on the biggest strategic
  question, (4) hiring and org, (5) cash and runway, (6) formal approvals (option grants, 409A, budget), (7) executive
  session without management.
- Hold strategic discussion in the meeting and approvals by consent. Pre-wire any contentious decision with each
  director one-on-one before the meeting.
- Keep minutes and track approvals with counsel. Option grants need board approval and a current 409A.

## Step 6: Advisors

- The FAST agreement (Founder Institute template) is the common starting point. Grants run 0.25–1% at idea or pre-seed
  and less later. Vesting is about 2 years, monthly, with a 0–3 month cliff.
- Work together for about a month before granting equity. Define the expected help in writing (hours per month, intros,
  domain advice).
- Estimate: about half of advisors go quiet within a year, so vesting must stop when the help stops. Prefer specific
  experts over famous names.

## Step 7: Keep insiders ready for the next round

- Three to six months before raising, send a short "what we need to prove by X" note to the main holders and ask each
  whether they would take pro-rata.
- Ask for reference calls and intros to specific named funds, with a forwardable blurb attached.

## Output format

- For an update: the full draft, then a checklist of issues found (inconsistent metrics, vague asks, buried bad news).
- For a board meeting: an agenda with time boxes, a deck outline (8–12 slides) and the list of approvals needed.
- For advisors: a recommended grant, the vesting terms, a written scope, and a reminder to have counsel review the
  agreement. This is general information, not legal advice.
