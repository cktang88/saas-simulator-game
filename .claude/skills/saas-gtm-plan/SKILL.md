# SaaS go-to-market plan: first 100 customers

You are helping a founder get their first 100 paying customers for a real SaaS product. Produce a focused, actionable plan with weekly numbers they can execute, not a list of every possible channel. Early GTM is about founder effort and learning, not efficient scale.

## Step 1: Gather context

Ask what is missing in one message:

1. Product in one sentence and the core outcome it delivers.
2. Ideal customer profile: role, company size/industry (B2B) or persona and situation (B2C); the trigger event that makes them look for a solution.
3. Business model: B2C, B2B SMB, or Enterprise. Who is the buyer vs the user?
4. Price and expected ACV (annual contract value per customer).
5. Current state: customers, waitlist, MRR, product readiness, any channel already tried with results.
6. Founder assets: network, audience/followers, communities, content skills, sales experience, budget for ads/tools.
7. Time available per week and team size.
8. Goal timeline (default: 90 days to a clear repeatable channel; 100 customers may take longer for higher ACV).

## Step 2: Choose the motion from ACV

Use this rule of thumb (approximate; adjust for complexity and buyer):

| ACV | Motion | Who sells | Typical first channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| < ~$1k/year | Self-serve | Product + content | Communities, SEO/content, short-form social, creators, launches, referral loops, app stores |
| ~$1k-$15k/year | PLG with inside-sales assist | Founder via video calls; product does the rest | Targeted outbound, communities, content, free trial with founder onboarding, integrations/marketplaces, partnerships |
| ~$15k+/year | Field / enterprise sales | Founder-led, later AEs | Warm intros, targeted account-based outbound, design partners, paid pilots, events, advisors/investors' networks |

Flag mismatches clearly (e.g. a $15/month product relying on demos, or a $50k product relying on Product Hunt). Note that 100 customers is a strong goal at low/mid ACV; for enterprise, 5-20 well-chosen customers may be the right first target, so adapt the goal and say so.

## Step 3: Pick 1-2 primary channels and 1 experimental channel

Score candidate channels 1-5 on: ICP concentration (are buyers gathered there?), founder advantage, cost, speed to signal, and scalability. Choose at most two primary channels plus one experiment. Explain why the others are deferred. Bias toward channels where the founder already has access.

## Step 4: Set weekly activity targets

Work backwards from the customer goal using funnel conversion assumptions. Label assumptions; replace them with real data after 2-3 weeks. Example structures:

**B2B outbound (SMB or Enterprise)**
- Build/enrich 100-200 ICP accounts per week; send 50-150 personalized 1:1 emails/LinkedIn messages per week with 2-3 follow-ups.
- Assume cold reply rates in the low single digits (better with sharp personalization and warm angles); meetings booked from a fraction of replies; close rates from qualified demos vary widely (often ~10-30% in SMB).
- Back-solve: customers needed / close rate = demos needed; demos / meeting rate = replies; replies / reply rate = touches.
- Enterprise: 10-30 target accounts at a time, multi-threaded (champion, economic buyer, technical evaluator); qualify with MEDDICC; propose paid pilots with written success criteria and a 30-90 day timeline; prepare for security questionnaires (SOC 2 is often requested).

**B2C / self-serve**
- Content: e.g. 5-7 short-form videos or 2-3 SEO articles per week, each testing a different hook; post consistently for at least 8-12 weeks before judging.
- Communities: answer 5-10 relevant questions per week; follow self-promotion rules.
- Creators: contact 10-20 micro-creators per week; offer affiliate or flat-fee deals; track unique codes/links.
- Referral: two-sided incentive; measure invites per user and invite conversion (k-factor = invites per user x invite conversion).
- Paid tests: small budgets ($10-$50/day) per ad set; kill anything whose CAC payback is far beyond target after enough spend to read.

**PLG-assist (SMB)**
- Founder personally onboards every new trial in week 1 (email + offer of a 15-minute call).
- Define product-qualified leads (e.g. invited 3+ teammates, hit a usage limit) and reach out within 24 hours.

## Step 5: Provide templates

Tailor to the user's ICP and product (fill in specifics, keep placeholders where unknown):
- Cold email/LinkedIn message (under ~120 words, one specific pain, one proof point, one clear ask) plus 2 follow-ups and a polite break-up.
- Community post that gives value first (lessons, template, data) with a soft mention.
- Launch post for Product Hunt / Show HN if relevant (humble, specific, demo link).
- Onboarding email sequence: welcome (day 0), activation nudge (day 1-2), case/use example (day 4), check-in from founder (day 7), trial ending (2 days before).
- Discovery call outline: context, current process, pain/cost, decision process, next step.
Remind the user to follow anti-spam and privacy laws (CAN-SPAM, GDPR, CASL, etc.) and to honor opt-outs.

## Step 6: Metrics to track weekly

Keep a simple spreadsheet or CRM. Track:
- Activity: touches sent, posts published, calls held.
- Funnel: reply rate, meetings booked, demos, trials started, activation rate, trial-to-paid, close rate.
- Outcomes: new customers, new MRR, CAC by channel (include founder time at a market rate), sales cycle length.
- Quality: 30/60/90-day retention of new customers by channel, top objections, reasons for lost deals.
Benchmarks to compare against (typical mid-2020s ranges, vary widely): opt-in trial to paid ~10-25% for B2B SMB, freemium to paid ~2-5%, CAC payback ideally under ~12 months for SMB and ~12-24 months for enterprise, LTV:CAC of 3 or more.

## Step 7: The 90-day plan

Output a table by two-week sprint:
- **Weeks 1-2 (setup)**: sharpen ICP and messaging using customer quotes; build lists; set up CRM/tracker and analytics; write templates; start activity at target volume.
- **Weeks 3-4 (first signal)**: run full volume; hold every call yourself; log objections; iterate subject lines, hooks and landing page weekly.
- **Weeks 5-6 (double down)**: compare channels by cost per customer and early retention; reallocate effort to the winner; kill or redesign the loser; ask happy customers for referrals and testimonials.
- **Weeks 7-8 (systemize)**: document the playbook that works (scripts, sequences, qualification criteria); add case studies; consider tools or a part-time helper for list building.
- **Weeks 9-10 (expand)**: test one adjacent segment or a second channel; tighten onboarding to raise activation.
- **Weeks 11-12 (review)**: report customers, MRR, CAC, payback and retention by channel; decide what to scale, and whether you have a repeatable motion worth hiring for.
For each sprint give numeric targets (activities and outcomes) derived from Step 4.

## Pitfalls to call out

- Spreading across too many channels; depth beats breadth early.
- Hiring salespeople before the founder has closed customers with a repeatable pitch.
- Launch spikes mistaken for a growth strategy.
- Optimizing acquisition while activation or retention is broken; check retention by channel.
- Mass, unpersonalized cold email (hurts deliverability and reputation).
- Vanity metrics (followers, sign-ups) instead of paid, retained customers.

## Rules

- Never invent results or statistics; label all conversion numbers as assumptions to be replaced by the user's data.
- Keep the plan specific to the user's ICP: name real types of communities, roles and trigger events, and tell them to verify specific venues exist and allow promotion.
- End with the three actions to take this week.
