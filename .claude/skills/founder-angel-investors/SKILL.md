# Raising from angels (late 2026)

You are helping a real founder raise from angels. Use the data in `docs/research/angels-and-landscape-2026.md` and `docs/research/fundraising.md`. Archetype behaviour is in `public/data/investor-archetypes.js`. Never invent an investor's name, thesis, portfolio or check size. Only work with names the user gives you or can verify, and label every estimate. This is not legal, tax or investment advice. Send the founder to a startup lawyer before signing and to a tax adviser for QSBS/SEIS questions.

## Step 1: Decide whether angels are the right capital

Ask for: MRR and growth, gross margin after inference, burn and runway, team size, the target amount, any money already committed, the incorporation (Delaware C-corp, UK Ltd, other), and the founder's goal (seed-strap to profit, or a venture path).
- **Angels-only round ($100K–$1M):** fits seed-strapping, solo founders and tiny teams with early revenue. No lead is needed if one angel anchors.
- **Angels plus an institutional lead:** fits rounds of $1M+. Get the lead first, because most angels follow.
- **Not angels:** if MRR is ≥$15K and the need is working capital, compare revenue-based financing (founder-exit-and-capital skill).
- Base rates to share: the median angel check is about $22–25K, angels make a median of about 2 new investments a year, and they fund about 1 in 4.4 opportunities they look at (UNH CVR 2024). A $500K angel round therefore usually means 10–25 checks.

## Step 2: Pick the angel types and build the list

Match the archetype to the company and say why:
- **Ex-founder operator angels ($10–50K):** decide on the founder and on selling ability. Fast, and they follow a lead.
- **AI-lab and AI-unicorn engineer angels ($10–100K):** have tender liquidity from 2025–26. They demand evals, inference margins and a real moat, and they dislike wrappers.
- **Big-tech operators ($5–25K):** slow but useful for enterprise intros.
- **Indie/bootstrapper angels ($5–25K):** like profitable solo SaaS and dislike high caps.
- **Super angels ($100–500K):** can anchor a round and decide within days, but are reached almost only through warm intros.
- **Syndicate leads (SPV $100K–1M):** need a lead and fixed terms first. SPVs take 2–4 weeks to fill and leads take 20% carry.
- **Angel groups ($50–500K):** take 1–3 months, prefer caps under ~$12M, often want info rights and an observer seat, and lean toward life sciences.
- **Scouts ($25–100K of a fund's money):** quick, but a scout check does not guarantee the fund.
- **Solo GPs ($100–500K):** thesis-specific, can lead, and resist caps above ~$20M.
- **International:** UK angels usually need SEIS/EIS advance assurance (SEIS company cap £250K; EIS limits doubled from April 2026).

Build 40–80 names in tiers. **Tier A** has a warm path and a thesis or domain match. **Tier B** is a fit but cold. **Tier C** is practice. Qualify each angel by asking: Do they still write checks (an investment in the last 12 months)? What is their check size? Do they invest at this stage and in this sector? Do they lead or follow? What do they help with? Put the list in a table with columns: name, type, check, lead/follow, path, evidence of recent activity, next step.

## Step 3: Get intros

- Ask existing investors, customers, portfolio founders of the target angel and accelerator peers. Warm intros convert to meetings at ~20–50%. Generic cold email gets a 1–5% reply rate.
- Use the **double opt-in**. Write a forwardable blurb of under 120 words: a one-liner, 2–3 proof points with numbers, the round (amount, instrument, cap, who's in), and why this angel specifically. The connector asks the angel first, then introduces.
- X/LinkedIn DMs work only when the founder has a visible public build record. Make them 3 sentences and specific, with a link to a demo.

## Step 4: Write the outreach

For a cold email or DM, write:
1. A subject that states the traction ("$18K MRR, solo, AI bookkeeping for dentists — raising $600K").
2. Line 1: why them (a portfolio company, a post they wrote, domain overlap). Make it true and specific.
3. Line 2: what it is, with one number.
4. Line 3: the round, its terms and its momentum.
5. Line 4: one small ask (a 20-minute call, or "can I send the 2-min demo?").

Send 2–3 follow-ups 4–7 days apart, each adding new information (a new customer, MRR growth, a commitment). Reject drafts that use hype ("revolutionary", "disrupt"), a top-down TAM, attachments over 10 MB, or mass-merge tells.

## Step 5: Prep and run the call (20–30 minutes)

- Prepare a 2-minute story (problem, earned insight, why now), a live demo or Loom, a one-page metrics sheet (MRR, growth, retention, gross margin after inference, burn) and the round terms.
- Rehearse the questions each archetype asks (see `questions` in the JS file). Expect: "what happens when the model provider ships this?", "inference cost per customer?", "who else is in?", "what if you never raise again?", "how can I help?" and "is this QSBS-eligible?".
- Close the call with a direct ask: "We're closing $X on [date]. Would you like to be part of it at $Y?" Note the angel's stated next step and send a follow-up email the same day.
- If the founder wants a role-play, play the archetype persona realistically: busy, skeptical and specific. Grade the founder afterward on clarity, evidence, handling of objections and the ask.

## Step 6: Set terms

- Use the YC **post-money SAFE** (81% of SAFEs are post-money; 93% are capped). Benchmark the cap against Carta Q2 2026 medians: <$250K round ~$8–10M; $250K–1M ~$10M; $1–2.4M ~$15M; $2.5M+ ~$30M. Show the dilution: each SAFE's ownership = check ÷ post-money cap.
- **MFN:** give it only on uncapped SAFEs or to the earliest checks before a cap is set, and limit it to economic terms.
- **Pro-rata side letter:** reserve it for meaningful checks (e.g. ≥$100K) or the anchor. Never grant it to all angels by default.
- **Info rights:** promise a monthly or quarterly update. Grant formal information rights or an observer seat only to an angel group or lead writing a large check.
- Refuse side letters with board seats, vetoes, ROFR on future rounds, or super pro-rata for small checks.
- Raise the "SAFE that never converts" question openly with seed-strappers. Discuss conversion-on-sale and conversion-on-dividend terms with counsel.

## Step 7: Compliance checks

- **Accredited investors only** for a normal Reg D round: $200K income ($300K joint), $1M net worth excluding the home, or a Series 7/65/82 licence. Use 506(b) (no public solicitation) unless the founder posts publicly about the raise. In that case use 506(c) with verification. A $200K+ minimum check plus representations can serve as verification under the SEC's March 2025 no-action letter. The INVEST Act is not law as of Oct 2026.
- **QSBS:** for US C-corp stock issued after July 4, 2025, the exclusion is 50% at 3 years, 75% at 4 and 100% at 5, with a $15M cap and a $75M gross-asset limit. Whether a SAFE starts the clock is unresolved, so say so and refer the founder to a tax adviser.
- File Form D within 15 days of the first sale, and check state blue-sky notices.

## Step 8: Close and manage

- Set a close date and a minimum. Start collecting signatures and wires once the first anchor commits (e-sign SAFEs; track them in a SAFE ledger or cap-table tool). Keep momentum: send weekly "who's in" updates during the raise.
- After closing, send a monthly update: metrics, wins, lowlights, and 1–3 specific asks (intros, hires, customers). Angels follow on mainly when they see consistent updates. Only ~33% of angels make follow-on investments.
- Keep a list of each angel's strengths and use them. Recruit the two best as advisors without extra equity unless the work is real (0.1–0.25%, vesting).

## Output format

1. Path verdict: angels-only, angels plus lead, or another source of capital.
2. A tiered angel list template with qualification columns.
3. An intro blurb, a cold email and a DM, all ready to send.
4. A call prep sheet with the likely questions and model answers.
5. A term sheet summary (cap, MFN, pro-rata, info rights) with a dilution table.
6. A compliance checklist and a 6-week close calendar.
