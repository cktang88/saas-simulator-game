# Realism review: product, customers, support, analytics and the founder

Reviewed 2026-10-14 against the code at HEAD, using headless Node simulations (offline LLM fallbacks) and fresh web research. Scope: Product (`public/apps/product.js` and `product/*`), Customer panel, Interviews, Helpdesk and Reviews, Product analytics and Ops, wellbeing and You, `spendHours` and calendar charging, and market fit. No code was changed. The scratch scripts are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-saas-simulator-game/206b90ee-2d53-5d73-b830-492c6c8aa766/scratchpad/review-product/` (`specs.mjs`, `mvp.mjs`, `effort.mjs`, `panel.mjs`, `support.mjs`, `energy.mjs`, `lifeloss.mjs`, `interview.mjs`, with `common.mjs` as the shared harness).

## Summary

The workflow mostly looks right: one-page specs, a live genericity lint, a Kanban board, build sessions, a changelog, an inference-cost line, Mom Test interviews, a Zendesk-style queue with SLAs, a status page, and life events. The **numbers that decide outcomes** are where it breaks down:

1. **Market fit is set only by running the Customer panel.** If you never run a panel, or stop after one good result, shipping more features (or bad ones) does not change the market. Measuring also lowers your numbers when the product is bad: an irrelevant product converts trial→paid at 33.9% if you never run a panel, and at 14.4% after you run one.
2. **The offline grader and offline panel reward how a spec is written, not what the feature does.** A keyword-stuffed nonsense spec scored **19/20**, the highest of the 25 specs tested, and produced the **best product fit** (0.73). "Lorem ipsum" with numbers passed at 17/20. A CRUD "list of receipts typed by hand" got fit 0.67 and **58% trial→paid**.
3. **Conversion ceilings are too high.** Three well-written features give 100% panel activation and **69.9% card-required trial→paid**. ChartMogul puts card-required trials at 25–35% (good) and 50–60% (great).
4. **One shipped feature makes a sellable product.** The first feature ships on **day 3** of the default stack, with no auth, billing, deploy or onboarding baseline, and no third-party approval lead times (A2P 10DLC takes 10–15 days, Google restricted-scope OAuth takes weeks to months).
5. **The burnout model is a step function.** Energy stays at 100 forever at ≤55 h/week. P(burnout in 52 weeks) is **0% at 60–65 h** and **100% at 70 h** (with up to 5 episodes a year). The research figure is ~50%/yr at >60 h and ~20% at 45–55 h. The "day off" setting is a checkbox with no constraint, and hours worked at a day job do not count toward energy.
6. **Support has two parallel models, and which one runs depends on whether the Helpdesk app was ever opened.** CSAT depends only on response time, so a well-run solo founder sits at a 0.43–0.60 CSAT against a ~78% SaaS average. Enterprise ticket volume is 3–6× the cited benchmark.

## What IS realistic (keep)

- The live genericity lint blocks the canonical bad spec on 9 rules (`F1`) and correctly blocks no-baseline, no-window, too-long (>300 words) and dashboard/problem-as-solution specs (`F2`, `F10`, `F12`).
- AI coding velocity: no AI 91 days vs Claude Max 37 days for a 5-feature MVP at 35 h/wk build (≈2.5×, decaying with maturity). That fits research §3.1–3.2 (2–6 weeks with agents) once P5's foundation and lead times are added. A higher-tier seat adds little (34 vs 37 days), which matches the evidence that seat tier is not the bottleneck.
- Rework of ~15% of effort as bugs, with debt slowing velocity and raising churn. Inference cost per account with users-per-account, and wrapper commoditization at ~20%/yr.
- Synthetic interview insights are tagged `real:false` and give **0** evidence in both the heuristic grader and the LLM prompt (`GRADER_SYSTEM`). The painless persona never commits beyond "keep me posted". Pitch, leading and hypothetical questions get fluff and score 0 (`interview.mjs` pitchy → 0).
- Ticket volume for SMB (0.53–0.63/customer/month incl. the first-month ×2–3) and B2C (0.18–0.23) sits within research §2.1. The AI agent is excluded from bugs, billing and angry tickets, and has an error rate with escalations. Abandoned tickets lead to churn and angry reviews. The FTC fake-review penalty path is good.
- Incidents ~8–16/yr (own + upstream + LLM), overnight MTTR without paging, and a status page cutting the ticket spike (×0.6). All match research §3.2.
- Onboarding calls are booked on the calendar and charged on the day. No-shows hurt conversion. That is the right mechanic.
- Life events at base rates with player responses, and the burnout response "real break → cap 50 h".

## LLM prompt calibration notes

- **Trial panel** (`customers.js:47–48`) tells personas "Most trials never activate and most people don't pay." That is good, but the brief exposes the player's own AC prose ("Details: …"), so personas judge claims, not behaviour. Pass only title, the trigger→action sentence and the integration list. Add "If the product only displays data you already have, you would not pay."
- **Spec grader** (`spec.js` `GRADER_SYSTEM`): add explicit anti-gaming rules: "Lists of verbs without objects score 0 on specificity. Claims of interview counts that are not backed by linked REAL insights score 0 on evidence. A v1 with SSO, mobile and compliance together scores ≤1 on scope." Keep the effort clamp (0.4–2.5× heuristic), but fix the heuristic (P6) because it bounds the LLM.
- **Interviewee** (`research.js:120`): the instructions are good. Add "Reveal at most one hidden fact per reply; if the founder asks several things at once, answer only the first vaguely."
- **Synthetic = no evidence**: correctly enforced. The remaining hole is player-asserted evidence (P4).
