# LLM judging rubrics: sales and customer conversations (2026)

Pattern: the model scores observable criteria 1-5 against 1/3/5 anchors and quotes its evidence; the game applies fixed weights (`public/core/rubric.js`). Role-play personas get the criteria as "what makes me respond or concede", not as a grader voice. Claims marked [unverified] come from secondary or vendor write-ups; [design] marks game assumptions. Existing operator-tier research is reused: `docs/research/operators/o1-sales-success.md`, `docs/research/sales-and-research.md` section 4.

Shared anti-gaming rules for every judge: (1) score only what is on the page, missing is low; (2) length is not quality: past the stated ideal band extra words lower the score; (3) stuffed keywords, repeated phrases or a pasted list of "right" questions score as the underlying behaviour, not the words (a criterion needs a quote that shows the behaviour in context); (4) generic praise or claims with no checkable noun (number, name, date, feature) cap the criterion at 2.

## 1. cold_email_judge (`sales/jobs.js`, recipient panel)
Judges: a cold email or follow-up as the recipient. Prompt gap: "judge it honestly" with four loose 1-10 fields. Output feeds `judgeToQuality` and the sequence step quality.

Benchmarks: reply rate platform average about 3.4%, top quartile about 5.5% [unverified, Instantly 2026 via https://mailshake.com/blog/cold-email-response-rate/]. Lavender, 231,818 emails to 4 Feb 2026: A-graded emails lift reply rate 58% (Operations, 5.4%) to 79% (Finance); only 6.1% of Finance emails earn an A; technical buyers 5.2% (https://www.lavender.ai/blog/the-cold-email-benchmark-report, Mar 2026). Length: reply rate falls past roughly 100 words (Gong, 28M emails, secondary [unverified]); a 25-50 word band for first touches [unverified, attributed to Lavender]; a SIGIR 2017 email-response study peaks near 40 words [unverified, secondary]. Subject length: sources disagree (1-4 vs 3-6 words, others find no effect) [unverified], so subject length is not scored. Reading-level claims could not be sourced; the criterion uses plain language only.

Criteria (weights in code):
- relevance (w2): 1 = pitch could go to any company; 3 = right role and problem area, generic reason; 5 = names a problem this role in this company plausibly has, visible on their site or role.
- specificity (w2): 1 = only merge tags (name, company); 3 = one true fact about them, not tied to the offer; 5 = a verifiable fact about them (page, hire, launch) that explains why this offer, and could not be sent to a different company.
- single_ask (w1.5): 1 = no ask, or 2+ asks; 3 = one ask but large (30-minute demo) or no reason given; 5 = one low-friction ask (interest question, 15 minutes) tied to a stated outcome.
- brevity_plain (w1): 1 = over 150 words, feature list or jargon; 3 = 100-150 words or some filler; 5 = about 40-100 words, plain words, first line about them not the sender.
- credibility (w1.5): 1 = hype, unproven claims, AI-sounding flattery ("impressed by your passion"); 3 = neutral, no proof; 5 = one concrete proof (named result, number, comparable customer) and nothing the product cannot do.
Anti-gaming: personalization must reference something checkable on the supplied site excerpt; a name or company merge tag only is specificity 1; padding beyond 100 words lowers brevity_plain.

## 2. prospect_thread_reply (`sales/jobs.js`, role-play)
Judges nothing; the prospect persona replies. Gap: persona has hidden facts but no model of what moves it. Added "what makes you answer or go quiet" (not a grader): answers a question you asked, proposes a concrete low-effort next step, uses your own words from earlier, did not repeat an ask you already declined, did not pitch before understanding. Pushy repetition after a no raises `unsubscribe`. `interest` (0-10) anchored: 0-2 = ignored the point or pushed; 5 = relevant but unproven; 8+ = specific to their situation with an easy next step. Source: Mom Test principles (Fitzpatrick 2013), o1 section 2 traps (repeat touches add no replies and raise complaints).

## 3. live_sales_call (`calls.js`, role-play)
Persona only. Added behaviour rules from Gong's finding that 11-14 targeted questions correlate with success and 3-4 problems surface on good calls (https://www.gong.io/blog/deal-closing-discovery-call, modified 4 Mar 2026): reveal one hidden fact per well-aimed question about the past; answer vague or hypothetical questions vaguely; get shorter and cooler after a monologue, a pitch before pain, or stacked questions; warm up when the seller repeats your words back; agree a next step only if it is concrete and dated. `engagement` anchored 0-3 / 4-6 / 7-10 to those behaviours.

## 4. call_scorecard (`calls.js`, grader)
Judges a discovery transcript. Gap: "strict coach" with a single 1-10 `discoveryQuality`. Benchmarks: 11-14 questions, 3-4 problems (Gong above); won deals about 57% rep talk vs 62% on lost [unverified]; decision-maker involved in first two stages lifts win rate 55% (Ebsta/Pavilion 2025 GTM Benchmarks, Apr 2025, https://www.ebsta.com/news-updates/ebsta-unveils-2025-gtm-benchmarks-report/, vendor release); Mom Test (Fitzpatrick 2013).
Criteria:
- pain_depth (w3): 1 = never asked about the problem, or only hypothetical; 3 = asked what the problem is, got a surface answer; 5 = got a specific recent incident ("last time") and what they tried.
- question_craft (w2): 1 = leading, hypothetical or compliment-fishing questions, or under 3 questions; 3 = 5-10 open questions, some follow-ups; 5 = 11-14 open past-behaviour questions with follow-ups that quote the prospect's previous answer; no stacked questions.
- listening (w1.5): 1 = founder over 60% of words or monologues over 90 words; 3 = 45-60%; 5 = under 40% and room after answers.
- quantified_impact (w2): 1 = no numbers; 3 = one figure (hours or dollars) not tied to a cost; 5 = cost in dollars and time per occurrence plus the consequence of doing nothing.
- stakeholders_process (w1.5): 1 = never asked who decides or how they buy; 3 = learned the signer's name or the timeline; 5 = signer, budget, process, paperwork and alternatives (including do nothing) all on record.
`discoveryQuality` (1-10) becomes 2 x weighted score when the live model scores; the offline fallback keeps its conservative formula. Anti-gaming: a question counts only with the prospect's answer present; stacked questions count once; "send me info" is not a next step.

## 5. renewal_negotiation (`success.js`, role-play)
Persona only; the game decides accept, counter or walk (`proposeRenewal`). Added "what makes me concede or dig in": a concession is traded for something (longer term, reference, case study) rather than given; an uplift tied to value or usage evidence I can see; acknowledgement of my real complaints (open bugs, slow support) before asking for more money; no irritators ("generous", "fair offer") [Huthwaite/Rackham 1978: skilled negotiators about 2 irritators per hour of speaking vs nearly 11 for average, secondary]; folding at the first push signals the price was inflated, so the customer asks for more. Uplift guidance stays: 5-10% normal, 15%+ aggressive. SaaS Capital 2025 NRR medians by ACV are in o1 section 5.

## 6. contract_redlines (`legal/llm.js`, counsel persona + grader)
Judges the vendor's clause positions and cover note as customer counsel. Benchmarks: Common Paper Cloud Service Agreement v1.0 general cap is a multiple of fees paid or payable in the prior 12 months with a "greater of" floor (cover page leaves values blank); a higher cap for increased claims (privacy/security, confidentiality) and unlimited only for gross negligence or willful misconduct in security breach; in Common Paper's own data of 10,000+ contracts, 5% of sales contracts include an increased cap and of those 82% use 5x (vendor statistic, indicative) (https://commonpaper.com/standards/cloud-service-agreement/1.0/cover-page-and-standard-terms-pdf, https://help.commonpaper.com/en/articles/6535163-increased-and-unlimited-claims-optional). Bonterms equivalent not fetched [unverified].
Criteria:
- market_standard (w2.5): 1 = accepts uncapped or "any claims" exposure, or strikes standard terms; 3 = mixed; 5 = every position inside Common Paper-style norms (12-month fees cap, super cap for data, IP indemnity).
- reasoned (w2): 1 = bare refusal or no cover note; 3 = reasons on some clauses; 5 = a one-line reason on each contested clause that names the standard or the real constraint.
- trade_offs (w1.5): 1 = concedes or refuses everything, nothing offered back; 3 = some alternatives; 5 = offers a capped or scoped alternative that meets the customer's stated fear (give-get).
- honorable (w2): 1 = commits to what cannot be delivered (EU-only everywhere, no subprocessors); 3 = commits with caveats; 5 = commits only to what the vendor can prove today (SOC 2 status, DPA, subprocessor list).
- responsiveness (w1): 1 = ignores what the customer asked; 5 = answers each ask directly.
Game: `dealProbability` = 0.5 x model estimate + 0.5 x mapped rubric ((weighted - 1) / 4, clamped 0.05-0.95). Anti-gaming: citing "Common Paper" without a matching position earns no credit; conceding everything scores low on honorable and trade_offs.

## 7. battlecard_review (`marketworld/llm.js`, rep or buyer panel)
Judges internal battlecards and public vs/alternatives pages. Klue's structure is Fact-Impact-Act with talk tracks and proof points per objection; win-loss interviews feed the content; landmine questions surface competitor weaknesses (https://klue.com/blog/sales-battlecard-template-objection-handling-and-counterpoints; one-page layout and the Acknowledge-Praise-Limit-Return pattern are third-party [unverified]).
Criteria: accuracy (w2): 1 = unsourced claims, exaggerations; 3 = mostly checkable; 5 = each claim has a number, date or feature a buyer can verify. differentiation (w2): 1 = "we are better/faster/cheaper"; 3 = a feature list; 5 = 1-3 differences tied to a buyer situation (when we win). fairness (w1.5): 1 = trash talk; 3 = no mention of rival strengths; 5 = says where the rival is better and when to choose them. actionability (w2): 1 = description only; 3 = either talk track or questions; 5 = for likely objections a talk track, a discovery or landmine question, and a proof point. proof (w1): 1 = none; 5 = named customer quote, dated price comparison, or screenshot reference. Game: old fields derive from the rubric (x2); trash talk still penalises.
Anti-gaming: pasted competitor feature lists with no buyer situation cap differentiation at 2; bulk length with no questions scores actionability 1.

## 8. demo_video_script (`website/demo.js`, buyer-side reviewer)
Judges a demo script. Benchmarks: Wistia engagement is flat from 1 to 5 minutes and drops sharply after 5; under 1 min suits awareness, 1-5 educate, 5-30 convert; engagement down 7% vs 2023 (https://wistia.com/blog/optimal-video-length, https://wistia.com/blog/insights-state-of-video-report; one-minute 50% vs over-five 38% is from a secondary summary [unverified]). Hook-specific data could not be sourced [unverified]; the hook criterion is [design]. Length stays game-computed in `scriptQuality` (ideal 60-120 s, [design]).
Criteria: hook (w2), problem_first (w1.5), shows_product (w3), outcome (w1.5), cta (w1), plain_language (w1). Anchors: hook 1 = starts with logo or "Hi, I'm"; 3 = names the product category; 5 = first sentence names the viewer and a pain in their words. problem_first 1 = feature first; 5 = a concrete pain moment before any product. shows_product 1 = claims only; 3 = 1-2 screens; 5 = 3+ concrete clicks in the real shipped product in one task. outcome 1 = none; 5 = a number or before/after the viewer gets. cta 1 = none or several; 5 = one next step. plain_language 1 = buzzwords (seamless, AI-powered); 5 = words the viewer uses. Anti-gaming: repeated words are caught by the fallback's distinctness check; unshipped features are penalised by the game.

## 9. channels_reply (`channels/judge.js`, partner persona)
Persona writes an email consistent with a decision the game already made. Gap: replies are generic. Added: the reply cites the one concrete thing in the proposal that drove the decision (an accept names what was specific or relevant; a decline names what was missing: audience fit, effort asked of them, no clear benefit to them); no invented numbers; max 120 words. Source: partner outreach norms in `docs/research/channels-partnerships-pricing.md`.
