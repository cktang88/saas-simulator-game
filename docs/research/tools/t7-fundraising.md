# T7: Fundraising and Investor Relations tools for small startups (2026)

Research date: 2026-10-14. Most sources are vendor blogs, competitor comparisons or undated aggregators. Prices conflict across sources, so every price below should be treated as a rough range until checked on the vendor's pricing page. Labels: [design] = my estimate, [unverified] = secondary or vendor-only claim.

Gaps: no usable 2026 data on Pitch.com or Figma Slides pricing; no Airtable-specific pipeline template found; no survey on what founders track in pipelines or how they handle passes; SBIR deadlines not checked.

## 1. Top tools by stage

### Pre-revenue / idea

- **Free investor lists: OpenVC, Signal by NFX, vcsheet, hub.waveup.com.** Cited as the early-stage lists that reply to founders. OpenVC is described as a free, community-sourced database with 10,000+ verified profiles. Signal is "100% free… we will never charge for it" per NFX's own FAQ (https://signal.nfx.com/faq). Signal lets founders add investors to a Target Investor List, filter by sector, stage and investor quality, and authorize Gmail to find existing contacts (https://products.nfx.com/en/articles/4809069-how-do-founders-use-signal). The Signal investor count is reported inconsistently (18,830 in one third-party listing, 10k+ in Draftboard's comparison), so treat it as approximate. Why founders pick it: free, and it finds warm-path names. [Sources: https://www.metal.so/guides/best-crms-built-for-founders-raising-venture-capital-in-2026/, 2026]
- **Free pipeline sheets: Dex template, Innmind (Google Sheets), Forecastr.** Innmind has built-in columns for SAFE/SAFT tracking, committed amounts and next steps. Forecastr tracks every interaction from cold intro to closed check. Dex's guide describes a pipeline as connected stages running from research to qualification to outreach (https://getdex.com/blog/investor-pipeline/; https://innmind.com/fundraising; https://www.forecastr.co/investor-pipeline-template). Why founders pick it: no subscription, and it matches what a first-time raiser needs. [design: this is the most common starting point for a sub-$100k raise]
- **SAFE generation: YC SAFE templates (free).** YC publishes cap-only, discount and MFN versions and advises consulting a lawyer licensed where the company is based (https://startupstarter.co/blog/early-stage-cap-table-tools; https://www.daphni.com/media-hub/the-simplest-way-to-issue-and-fund-safes; secondary). Widely used for pre-seed rounds [design].

### First customers (<$10k MRR), pre-seed / seed prep

- **Investor CRM: Foundersuite.** Kanban pipeline, investor database, investor updates, deck hosting with view tracking, and a "Get Intro" warm-intro finder. A reviewer says it is less suited to in-app email (https://www.capterra.com/p/144844/Foundersuite/; https://angelspartners.com/reviews/foundersuite/). Database size is reported as 216,000+ (vendor), 230,000+ (Capterra), and 141,000+ (Subscribed.fyi). Pricing conflicts: a free Basic tier capped at 75 investors per pipeline, paid tiers at $69, $99, $149/mo (Angelspartners); a Platinum tier at $134/mo flat (Capterra); annual Silver and Gold at $745 and $1,069 (ITQlick). Capterra rating 4.7/5 from 110 reviews. [Dates: reviews 2026]
- **Investor CRM: Visible (Visible for Companies).** Founder-side investor updates, KPI dashboards and fundraising pipeline. Pricing is not public. A VC Beast list gives a Startup plan at $149/mo and a Growth plan at $249/mo, but this is a single secondary blog [unverified]. Ellty (Jan 2026) says it requires an annual contract with no self-serve option and estimates $500–800/mo for early-stage startups [unverified]. Do not confuse it with "Visible for Investors," a separate investor-side product (https://www.g2.com/products/visible-for-investors/pricing; https://vcbeast.com/best-investor-update-tools).
- **Metal, Round Funded.** All-in-one fundraising CRMs, each ranking itself first in its own guides. Metal quotes $600/quarter for pre-seed; Round Funded lists $99/mo and a 60,000+ verified investor database (https://www.metal.so/guides/best-crms-built-for-founders-raising-venture-capital-in-2026/; https://www.roundfunded.com/en/blogs/best-investor-crm-for-founders-2026). [unverified, vendor claims]
- **Investor database: Crunchbase Pro.** About $49/mo annual or $99/mo monthly, with funding-round data, saved-search alerts and a 2,000-row monthly export cap. Contact unlocks are extra, $29–99/mo, and CRM integrations start at the Business tier (about $199/mo) (https://syncgtm.com/blog/crunchbase-pro-review-2026; https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/crunchbase). Sources conflict on whether pricing is per user or per account. [unverified]
- **Deck tools.** Gamma: free credits, Pro at about $15/mo removes branding (https://gamma.app/explore/content/guides/free-pitch-deck-tools-startups; https://is4.ai/blog/our-blog-1/top-10-ai-presentation-tools-2026-316). Beautiful.ai: Pro about $19/user/mo. Slidebean: built for startup decks. Pitch.com and Figma Slides: nothing usable found [gap].
- **Data room and view tracking: DocSend vs Papermark.** DocSend is per-seat on lower tiers, with a 14-day trial and plans starting around $15/user/mo. Its Advanced and Data Rooms plans include 3 users. Papermark lists Data Rooms at €99/mo, with NDA gating and custom domains on its business tiers. Price figures conflict across Papermark, Orangedox and Pitchwise (https://www.ellty.com/blog/docsend-pricing; https://www.papermark.com/blog/papermark-vs-docsend.md; https://www.pitchwise.se/blog/papermark-pricing-review-2026). [unverified; competitor claims]

### Scaling ($10k–$100k MRR, seed to Series A)

- **Priced-round cap table and SAFE tooling: Carta.** Plans are Launch, Build and Grow with custom pricing. Launch includes SAFE modeling, cap table management and fundraising benchmarks. A third-party guide says the free tier covers up to 25 stakeholders and under $1M raised, and the median paid plan costs about $14,725 per year (https://capterra.com/p/220278/Carta/pricing/; https://carta.com/sg/en/equity-management/cap-table/safes/). Carta-reported data says SAFEs were about 93% of US pre-seed rounds on its platform in Q2 2026 (https://www.peony.ink/blog/safe-vs-convertible-note) [unverified; Carta-only sample].
- **Incorporation and paperwork: Clerky.** Online legal service for Delaware C-corps. Reviewers cite $427 and $819 price points without saying which plan each applies to. About half of YC companies in each batch use it (https://www.flowjam.com/blog/clerky-review-2026-complete-guide-pricing-alternatives). [unverified]
- **Syndicates, SPVs and rolling funds: AngelList.** (Also see Carta's and Clerky's roles above.) Standard SPV costs about $8,000 setup plus a $2,000 state fee, with an $80,000 minimum raise. Follow-on SPVs are about $5,000 plus $2,000 with a $50,000 minimum. Leads typically charge 20% carry. Sources disagree on whether AngelList takes a 5% platform carry, and the narrower reading (only deals found through its distribution products) is more likely. Rolling-fund fees are quoted privately (https://www.allocations.com/insights/angellist-fees-in-2026-the-complete-breakdown-spv-setup-carry-and-add-ons; https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/angel-investing/angellist-explained-how-it-works). [unverified; Allocations is a competitor]
- **Revenue-based financing.** Lighter Capital: funds software companies with at least $200k ARR, facilities up to about $4M, no equity, repayment tied to revenue over 3–5 years (https://azbigmedia.com/business/top-10-revenue-based-financing-firms-in-2026/; https://founderpath.com/compare/best-revenue-based-financing-companies). Pipe: a marketplace that buys recurring-revenue contracts at a discount, with terms around 12 months and funding in 2–5 days (https://startuprise.co.uk/best-revenue-based-financing-companies-in-2026/; https://founderpath.com/compare/founderpath-vs-capchase). Capchase: reportedly acquired Vartana in June 2025 and de-emphasized RBF; its effective cost runs about 10–15% a year on the discounted contract value (https://startupsavant.com/?p=19300; https://ghost-blog-x7ak.onrender.com/best-factoring-options-for-saas-startups/). Stripe Capital: offers are made by email based on Stripe volume and history. A typical offer is a flat fee on the advance, repaid as a fixed percentage of Stripe card sales. One example: $17,000 advance, $1,700 flat fee, 12% payment rate (https://docs.stripe.com/capital/servicing; https://help.hungryhungry.com/stripe-capital; the example is from a partner page). [unverified]
- **Investor updates.** Visible (above), Foundersuite's update feature, or plain email. Most founders use email for updates (no 2026 survey found) [design].

## 2. Core workflows (user stories)

1. **Build a target list from filters.** As a founder, I filter an investor database (Signal, Crunchbase, Foundersuite) by stage, sector, geography and check size, then save the matches to a target list. UI: filter panel, "save to list," a result count. Data: investor profile, stage, sector, last-known check.
2. **Find the warm path.** As a founder, I search an investor by name and see which of my contacts (imported from Gmail or LinkedIn) can introduce me. Foundersuite's "Get Intro" and Signal's Gmail link are the pattern. Data: contact graph, relationship strength.
3. **Move an investor through pipeline stages.** I drag a card on a Kanban board (Research, Intro sent, Meeting, Partner meeting, Term sheet, Passed, Closed). Each card holds the partner name, last touch date, next step and owner. Data: investor, stage, activity log.
4. **Log a meeting and the next step.** After a call, I record the date, attendees, questions asked, objections and the follow-up task. Data: activity record, linked task with due date.
5. **Send a deck and see who opened it.** I share a link (DocSend or Papermark, or Foundersuite's deck hosting) with NDA gating if required. I see view time per page and which investor opened it. Data: link, viewer, page-level analytics.
6. **Share the data room.** I upload folders (cap table, financials, contracts, IP assignments), set per-folder permissions, and check who downloaded what. Data: folder tree, permissions, access log.
7. **Model a SAFE or priced round.** I enter the cap, discount, MFN and pool size, then see dilution at a given raise. Carta's SAFE modeling or a Google Sheet does this. Data: instrument, cap, round size, pre- and post-money cap table.
8. **Send a monthly investor update.** I write metrics (MRR, burn, runway, hires), a lowlight, and specific asks (intros). The update goes to the investor list. Data: KPI snapshot, recipients list, send log, reply tracking.
9. **Track commitments and close the round.** I record each soft commit, check amount, instrument (SAFE or priced), and the expected close date, then compare the sum to the target. Data: commitment row, closing checklist.
10. **Run a syndicate or SPV.** I set up the SPV, invite LPs, collect commitments and send the closing docs. AngelList and Allocations do this. Data: LP list, commitment amount, accreditation status, SPV fee schedule.
11. **Apply for an accelerator or grant.** I fill in the application (company, traction, team, video), track the deadline, and record the decision. Data: application form, deadline, status.
12. **Compare revenue-based financing offers.** I enter the advance amount, flat fee or APR, repayment percentage and term, then compare total cost. Data: offer terms, repayment schedule, linked Stripe or accounting revenue.

## 3. Must-have features for a convincing clone

- **Investor database:** searchable by stage, sector, geography, check size, lead vs follow, last-active date. Each record has a name, firm, email, LinkedIn, notes, and a last-touch date.
- **Pipeline board:** Kanban and table views. Stage counts at the top. Filters for owner and next-step date. An overdue-follow-up list.
- **Warm-intro finder:** a contact graph, with the intro path shown as "Alice → Bob → investor."
- **Activity log:** every email, call and deck view appears on the investor record.
- **Deck and data room:** page-view analytics, NDA gate, per-viewer tracking, folder permissions, download log.
- **SAFE and round modeler:** cap, discount, MFN, pool, pre- and post-money cap table. Dilution table by round.
- **Investor update composer:** metric fields pulled from a dashboard or entered by hand, a send list, open tracking.
- **Commitment tracker:** commits per investor, check size, instrument, status (soft, signed, funded), total against target.
- **Reports and metrics:** pipeline by stage, conversion between stages, meetings per week, average days in each stage, total committed vs target, runway.
- **Integrations:** Gmail or Outlook (sync), Calendar, Stripe or accounting for revenue, data room link.

Key metrics founders watch: number of investors in each stage, meetings per week, reply rate on cold intros, days from first meeting to partner meeting, committed vs target, runway in months. [design]

## 4. What changed with AI in 2025–2026

- **Automated:** list building from natural-language prompts, investor matching (Signal, Metal, Round Funded), cold-email drafts, deck generation (Gamma, Beautiful.ai), summarizing a call transcript into a CRM note, drafting the monthly investor update from a metrics dashboard.
- **Still needs a human:** choosing which investors fit (the lists contain stale or wrong-stage names), any personal intro, negotiating caps and terms, deciding what to say in a lowlight, signing legal documents (the vendor-side guidance says to consult a lawyer for SAFEs).
- **Typical failure modes:** AI-generated investor matches with the wrong stage or geography; drafted emails that sound generic; stale contact data producing bounced emails; auto-summaries that record a "yes" that was really a "maybe"; investor-update drafts that round numbers the founder didn't check. ## 5. Common mistakes small founders make

- **Paying for an all-in-one CRM before the first meeting.** A free sheet (Innmind, Dex) covers the first 50 investors.
- **Buying a large investor database and emailing everyone.** A short list of 30–50 well-matched names beats a list of 3,000.
- **Starting the raise with no warm paths.** Cold outreach converts far worse than intros [design].
- **Treating view analytics as intent.** A deck opened twice is not a term sheet.
- **Letting the pipeline go stale.** Founders forget to update stages, so the board shows fake momentum. Use a weekly review with a set cut-off date.
- **Paying for a SAFE platform for a three-SAFE round.** A free YC template plus a lawyer's review is often enough; Carta's paid tiers fit priced rounds.
- **Using revenue-based financing against unstable revenue.** The flat fee or discount can equal a high annual cost, and repayment comes out of the same cash the business needs.
- **Joining an SPV without checking the fee stack.** Setup, state, admin and carry can add up beyond the headline fee.
- **Missing accelerator deadlines.** YC's Fall 2026 on-time deadline was reported as July 27, 2026, so the Fall batch is closed as of today; check ycombinator.com for the next one [unverified, aggregator source].
- **Sending a standard investor update only when raising.** Monthly updates keep angels engaged between rounds.
