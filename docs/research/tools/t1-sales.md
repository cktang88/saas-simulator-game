# Sales tools for small startups, 2026

Research date: 2026-10-14. Prices are per seat per month unless noted. Most price figures come from third-party aggregators and vendor-affiliated comparison blogs that disagree with each other, so they are marked [unverified]. Check vendor pricing pages before budgeting.

## 1. Top tools by stage

### Idea / pre-revenue
- **HubSpot CRM (free tier).** The most-used CRM in startup data. Zero's sample of 671 demo requests (Aug 2026) puts HubSpot at 31.1% of teams, about 38% adjusted for undercounting. Source: https://zero.inc/blog/what-crm-do-startups-use (vendor, biased toward teams switching CRMs). Free tier is forever-free. Why founders pick it: no cost to start, one place for contacts, deals and email logging. [unverified pricing]
- **Spreadsheet / Notion / Google Sheets.** Zero's sample puts spreadsheets third at 13.1%. Same source. [design: most pre-revenue founders start here]
- **Cal.com (free) or Calendly (free, one event type)** for booking calls. Calendly Free is $0 with 1 event type. Source: https://costbench.com/compare/calendly-vs-savvycal/ [unverified]
- **Granola or Fathom (free tiers)** for recording founder calls. Granola's free tier has unlimited meetings. Source: https://www.costbench.com/compare/granola-vs-fathom/ [unverified]

### First customers (under $10k MRR)
- **Attio.** Second in Zero's sample at 15.2%. Free tier, then Plus and Pro. One 2026 source gives Plus at $35 billed annually ($44 monthly) and Pro at $79 annually ($99 monthly), after a mid-2026 repricing; G2 still shows the older $29/$69. Seat caps are reported inconsistently. Source: https://marketbetter.ai/blog/attio-crm-pricing-breakdown-2026/ [unverified]. Why founders pick it: custom object model, a relationship-first layout.
- **Folk.** Starts at $24/user/month with no permanent free tier, per Dupple's comparison table. Source: https://dupple.com/learn/best-sales-crm-for-startups [unverified]. Used for contact-centric, LinkedIn-driven CRM and light outbound. No usage share found.
- **Pipedrive.** The common pick for sales-led teams; recommended in Parse's analysis of AI-search answers (https://www.parse.gl/prompts/p/in-your-opinion-whats-the-top-crm-choice-for-a-quickly-growing-technology-startup--ccc6e62b-b1b0-45ac-b1fa-66fecc0b5709). Price is unresolved: one source lists Essential $14–24, Advanced $24–49, Professional $50–60 per user per month, and another source says the lineup moved to Lite/Growth/Premium/Ultimate. CostBench reports a top-tier increase in April 2026 (https://costbench.com/changelog/pipedrive-price-increase-2026-04/). [unverified]
- **Apollo.io** for prospecting plus its built-in sequencer (see section 2).
- **HubSpot Quotes (native, free)** for proposals on simple pricing. Source: https://costbench.com/best/best-cpq-for-small-business-2026/ [unverified]

### Scaling ($10k–$100k MRR / seed–A)
- **HubSpot Sales Hub (Starter to Professional).** Adds sequences, call logging, custom pipelines, and reporting. Usually the choice once a team has reps or needs marketing automation.
- **Attio Pro** or **Pipedrive** at higher tiers, for multi-pipeline reporting and automation.
- **Salesforce.** 4.0% in Zero's sample. Used once an accounting, security or enterprise requirement appears. Entry pricing about $150–200 per user per month for CPQ-type editions per CostBench [unverified]. Most startups below Series A should not start here.
- **Gong** for call intelligence once there are 5+ reps. Estimated $1,200–2,400 per user per year plus a platform fee of about $5k–50k per year [unverified, Vendr-based estimates via https://coworker.ai/blog/gong-pricing]. The platform fee makes it a poor fit for solo or small teams.

### Prospecting and data
- **Apollo.io.** Free plus Basic about $49/user/month (annual), Professional about $79, Organization about $119 with a three-user minimum. Mobile and phone reveals cost credits (about 8 credits per phone). Overage around $0.20/credit. Source: https://www.zeliq.com/blog/apolloio-pricing; https://www.joinvalley.co/blog/apollo-pricing-and-credits-explained-2026 [unverified, conflicting free-tier credit counts]
- **Clay.** Old Starter/Explorer/Pro plans closed to new customers in 2026. Current self-serve plans are Free, Launch (about $167–185/month, 2,500 data credits), and Growth (about $446–595/month, 6,000 credits). Mid-month top-ups carry a surcharge. Sources: https://blog.gojiberry.ai/blog/clay-pricing; https://www.docket.io/resources/research/clay-pricing [unverified]. Used for waterfall enrichment and signal-based lists.
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator.** Core about $90–120/seat/month, Advanced about $150–160/seat/month, Advanced Plus quote-based. Source: https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/linkedin-sales-navigator-pricing; https://www.findymail.com/blog/linkedin-sales-navigator-pricing/ [unverified]. Bulk export breaks LinkedIn's terms.
- **ZoomInfo.** Not published. Estimated $15k–40k per year on contract, with all-in $30k–60k reported for teams [unverified, third-party estimates: https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/linkedin-sales-navigator-pricing]. Too expensive for most under-20-person startups; founders use Apollo or Clay instead.

### Sequencing and deliverability
- **Instantly.** Growth from about $47/month with unlimited mailboxes and warmup; Hypergrowth about $97 adds unified inbox. Source: https://instantly.ai/blog/instantly-vs-smartlead-lemlist-2026/ (vendor-authored) [unverified]. Chosen for simple setup and a polished operator experience.
- **Smartlead.** Base $39, Pro $94, Unlimited tiers $174 and $379 per month. Fair-use mailbox caps reported (100/300/800 by tier) by a competitor, not confirmed [unverified]. Chosen for API depth and warmup control.
- **Lemlist.** Per-user pricing: Email Pro about $63–79/user/month, Multichannel Expert about $87–109/user/month. Only one of the three with native LinkedIn automation. Source: https://www.smartreach.io/blog/instantly-vs-smartlead-vs-lemlist/ [unverified]
- **Apollo sequences.** Included with Apollo seats, so often used instead of a separate sequencer.

### AI SDRs
- **11x (Alice).** Reported at about $3,750/month billed annually on its Growth plan (about $45k/year), with managed mailboxes and onboarding. Reviewers say replies are weak and personalization is thin. Source: https://www.joinvalley.co/blog/artisan-vs-11x [unverified, vendor-competitor source].
- **Artisan (Ava).** Public plans of about $250–600/month were reported; a 2026 source says pricing moved behind a sales call with real deals reported at $1,500–5,000/month [unverified].
- **AiSDR** (about $250–900/month) and **Agent Frank** (about $499–599/month) are cited as lower-cost alternatives [unverified]. Piper was not found in this search.

### Call recording and intelligence
- **Granola.** Basic free with unlimited meetings; Business about $14/user/month; Enterprise about $35. Source: https://www.costbench.com/compare/granola-vs-fathom/ [unverified]. Notes-first, no bot joins the call.
- **Fathom.** Free tier; top paid tier rose from $28 to $29 (Feb 2026) and to $34 (June 2026) per CostBench changelog: https://costbench.com/changelog/fathom-price-increase-2026-06/ [unverified]. Lowest total cost at comparable tiers per CostBench.
- **Fireflies.ai.** Individual $18, Business $29 per user per month per CostBench; Vendr median is high for a small sample. [unverified]
- **Gong.** See section 1, scaling stage.

### Scheduling
- **Calendly.** Standard about $10–12/seat/month, Teams about $16–20/seat/month. Broadest integrations and round-robin routing. [unverified]
- **SavvyCal.** Basic and Premium about $12 and $20 per user per month. CRM integrations and routing are Premium. [unverified]
- **Cal.com.** Free for self-hosting; teams plan about $15–16/seat. [unverified]

### Proposals and e-signature
- **PandaDoc.** Bundles document building and e-sign. Essentials about $19/user/month; Business about $49–65/seat. Sources conflict. [unverified] Source: https://costbench.com/compare/docusign-vs-pandadoc/
- **DocuSign.** Standard about $25–30/user/month annual. Used mainly when counterparties expect it. [unverified]
- **Dropbox Sign (HelloSign).** Free entry tier; paid around $11–15/user/month. [unverified] Source: https://dupple.com/learn/best-e-signature-software
- **Cheap alternatives:** signNow about $8/user/month, SignWell $10/month, Proposify $19–49/month [unverified].

### CPQ basics
- **HubSpot Quotes.** Native, included with Sales Hub. Covers flat and tiered pricing, approvals, branded templates, and payments via HubSpot Payments or Stripe.
- **DealHub, Salesforce CPQ.** For complex catalogs and 20+ reps. Salesforce CPQ entry about $150/user/month [unverified]; DealHub median around $54k/year per one review site [unverified]. Implementation can run months.

## 2. Core workflows (weekly)

1. **Triage the inbound lead queue in the CRM.** Open the new-lead view, check source and form fields, assign or disqualify, set next-step date. Data: contact, company, source, lifecycle stage.
2. **Build a target list with filters and enrich it.** In Apollo or Sales Navigator, filter by title, headcount, industry, funding stage; export to Clay or the sequencer; enrich emails and phones and drop bounced rows. Data: account and contact records, credit balance.
3. **Check email deliverability before a send.** In Instantly or Smartlead, look at warmup score, bounce rate and spam-complaint rate for each mailbox; pause mailboxes that dropped. Data: sending domains, DNS status, daily send counts.
4. **Review and approve sequence steps and personalization.** Edit templates with merge fields, check reply and open rates per step, retire steps under 1% reply. Data: sequence step, variant, sends, replies, positive replies.
5. **Work the reply inbox.** Read positive replies, move the prospect to a meeting booked stage, log the note on the contact. Data: reply thread, sentiment tag, task.
6. **Review call recordings and action items.** In Granola, Fathom or Gong, skim the summary, tag objections and next steps, push fields to the CRM. Data: transcript, speaker, action items, deal ID.
7. **Run the pipeline review.** Walk each deal in the stage view; move stalled deals, update close date and amount, check forecast total. Data: stage, amount, close date, last activity.
8. **Send a proposal and collect signature.** Build from template in PandaDoc or HubSpot Quotes, set line items and discount, send, track views, then e-sign. Data: quote lines, discount, signer, status.
9. **Book and route meetings.** Share a scheduling link, handle reschedules, route to the right rep by round robin or by territory. Data: event type, host, invitee fields, booking status.
10. **Review metrics.** Check stage conversion, win rate, cycle length, and sequence reply rate. Data: deal count by stage, win rate, median days to close.

## 3. Must-have features for a convincing clone

- **Contacts and companies with custom fields**, dedupe, activity timeline, email logging.
- **Deal pipeline (kanban and table views)** with stages, amount, close date, owner, forecast category. HubSpot and Attio both show these.
- **Lead list builder** with filters (title, company size, tech stack, funding), enrichment columns, credit meter, CSV import and export.
- **Sequencer** with multi-step email and LinkedIn tasks, per-mailbox daily caps, pause on reply, A/B variants.
- **Mailbox health screen** with warmup status, bounce and complaint rates, SPF/DKIM/DMARC check.
- **Reply inbox (unibox)** that merges replies across mailboxes.
- **Call recording panel** with transcript, timestamps, summary, and CRM sync.
- **Booking page** with event types, availability windows, round-robin and buffer settings.
- **Proposal builder** with line items, discounts, approvals, e-sign and status tracking.
- **Reports:** pipeline by stage, weighted forecast, win rate, sales cycle, reply rate, bounce rate, cost per meeting.

## 4. What changed with AI in 2025–2026

- **Automated now:** list building and enrichment waterfalls (Clay), first-draft personalization, call summaries and CRM field capture (Granola, Fathom, Gong), meeting booking, and sequence sending with auto-pause on reply.
- **Still needs a human:** reply handling and objections, target selection, deal qualification, pricing and discount approval, and deliverability decisions (when to pause a domain).
- **Typical failure modes:** AI SDRs send generic personalization that reads as spam; reviewers measured only about 24% of emails with meaningful personalization in one test (https://www.joinvalley.co/blog/artisan-vs-11x) [unverified]. Enrichment returns stale or wrong emails and inflates bounce rates. Call summaries hallucinate next steps. Per-seat AI credits add cost on Gong and Clay [unverified].
- **Pricing trend:** AI SDR vendors moved to managed, high-ticket plans ($1.5k–5k/month reported), and several tools added usage credits.

## 5. Common mistakes

- **Over-tooling.** A pre-revenue founder running HubSpot, Apollo, Clay, Instantly, Gong and an AI SDR is paying about $600+/month before the first deal. [design]
- **Enterprise contracts early.** Gong's platform fee ($5k–50k/yr) and ZoomInfo's $30k+ contracts make no sense below about 5 reps [unverified].
- **Skipping deliverability setup.** Sending from the main domain, skipping warmup and sending over 50/day per mailbox gets the domain blacklisted. Use secondary domains and keep SPF, DKIM and DMARC aligned. [design]
- **Buying AI SDRs before the offer works.** A managed AI SDR amplifies a bad message; prove reply rates manually first. [design]
- **Per-seat tools for solo founders.** Lemlist's per-user pricing and Pipedrive's tier jumps punish teams that add seats quickly. [unverified]
- **Using scraped LinkedIn data.** Breaks LinkedIn's terms and can get the account restricted.
- **Keeping pipeline in a spreadsheet past 20 deals.** Stage dates and next steps drift, and forecast misses follow.
