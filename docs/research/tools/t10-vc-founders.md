# T10: VC-backed early-stage startups 2025-2026 (AI-native, tiny teams, fast ARR)

Research date: 2026-10-14. Confidence tags: [primary] = company statement or primary press; [secondary] = trade press or aggregator; [unverified] = only secondary or aggregator sources, or a claim I could not check against its original.

Method note: Most public ARR figures for private companies are founder statements relayed by press, or third-party estimates (Sacra, ARR Club, Latka). Team-size and time-allocation data are the weakest part of the record. Where a figure conflicts across sources, both are given.

## 1. Company cases

### Lovable (B2C/B2B "vibe coding" app builder, Sweden)
- Product: AI app and website builder from prompt. Mixed consumer and prosumer/SMB use.
- ARR path: $17M ARR about three months after launch; roughly 30,000 paying customers at that point; up to $2M new ARR per week [secondary, growthunhinged/thegrowthmind substacks, date not stated; treat as unverified]. TechCrunch (contains company statements) says Lovable previously reported $100M ARR in July, $200M in November, $300M in January, and $400M in February; the year is in the TechCrunch piece, linked at https://techcrunch.com/?p=3101477 [secondary, read via search snippet; full text not fetched].
- Team: about 15 people at the $17M point [secondary, unverified]; 18 employees in April 2025 per Techleap note (https://finder.techleap.nl/news/note/europe-s-fastest-growing-startup) [secondary]; about 40-45 at the $100M mark per Indie Hackers post (https://www.indiehackers.com/post/ONMaxjB3rix2PnBCnrDr, Aug 2025) and a second secondary source [unverified]; 146 employees at $400M per TechCrunch headline [secondary].
- Ratio: roughly $2M ARR per employee at the $100M mark, if the 45-person figure holds [unverified].
- GTM: self-serve PLG; prompt-to-app as the first-use experience. Founders Anton Osika (CEO) and Fabian Hedin [secondary].
- Ramp: Lovable appears on Ramp's May 2026 top-10 fastest-growing software vendors list (https://ramp.com/data/top-saas-vendors-on-ramp-may-2026) [secondary, via search summary; list contents not fetched].
- a16z Top 100 consumer AI (7th ed., reported 2026-10-05): Lovable at No. 8 on a paid U.S. consumer-spend ranking [secondary, aggregator ToolNavs/AIIDELIST; unverified].

### Bolt.new / StackBlitz (B2C/prosumer, PLG)
- Product: prompt-to-app builder on WebContainers.
- ARR: "$0 to $20M ARR in 60 days, with 15 people" per AI Engineer World's Fair 2025 talk title (https://www.ai.engineer/talks/s8RM8uYxkoY-bolt-new-we-scaled-0-20m-arr) [primary-ish, talk title; the talk itself not fetched].
- $40M ARR by March 2025 per Sacra (https://sacra.com/c/bolt-new/) and Panto (https://www.getpanto.ai/blog/?p=4508); Lenny's Newsletter describes $0 to $40M and 1M MAU in about five months (https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/inside-bolt-eric-simons) [secondary].
- Funding: $105.5M Series B led by Emergence and GV, about $700M valuation, January 2025 [secondary]. Sacra reports $135M total funding as of December 2025 [secondary].
- Team: under 20 during launch [secondary]; ToolJunction lists 20-50 later [secondary, unverified].
- Year-end 2025 ARR: not confirmed. The $100M ARR figure is a projection in ToolJunction and should not be cited as a result [unverified].

### Cursor / Anysphere (B2B developer tool, PLG to enterprise)

## 5. Common patterns

1. ARR numbers are mostly company statements relayed by press, plus Sacra-style estimates. Treat a single source as weak, and always cite the date a figure was reported, not the date it applies to.
2. Lean builds are real at the $1-100M stage. Bolt (15 people, $20M in 60 days), Lovable (about 15 to 45 people to $100M), and Gamma (about 50 people, profitable, $100M) show $1-2M ARR per head. Cursor's roughly 60 engineers at about $300M is the high end.
3. Growth at the top is funded and team-heavy. Harvey and Sierra show the other shape: $100-200M ARR with hundreds of staff, enterprise sales, and implementation. Decagon and Sierra valuations scaled faster than public ARR.
4. PLG and self-serve dominate the consumer and prosumer side: Lovable, Bolt, Gamma, Granola. Enterprise AI (Harvey, Sierra, Decagon) is founder-led and sales-heavy, though Sierra and Clay also have self-serve motions.
5. Valuations outrun disclosed revenue. Clay's valuation rose from $1.25B to $7.1B in about 18 months while its ARR was estimated between $100M and $150M. Treat multiples as market signal, not operating evidence.
6. AI spend is now a major software category in its own right. Ramp's data points to AI services, AI notetakers, and AEO as the fastest-growing purchases, with incumbents in project management and BI still leading but losing share.
7. The stack is small and goal-driven. Public guidance (Cut-the-SaaS, listicles) says buy one tool per function until traction; Ramp and Cledara numbers suggest small companies spend heavily on software per head.
8. Founder time-use data is missing from my sources. The most important question for the simulator, how founders split time between sales, product, support, and fundraising, needs primary interviews (podcasts, YC Startup School talks, Lenny's Newsletter, Sacra founder interviews) that I did not retrieve.
