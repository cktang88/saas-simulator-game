# Finance, accounting, banking and payments: founder tool landscape (2026)

Research date: 2026-10-14. Tags: [design] = my estimate; [unverified] = secondary or vendor-only claim. Vendor pricing changes often; re-check before using any price as a fact.

**Premise corrections found in research**
- Causal was not found to have shut down. Search results say it was acquired by Lucanet in October 2024 [unverified]; the Lucanet homepage fetch did not mention Causal.
- Runway (the FP&A product) was not shut down either. It rebranded as cfo.ai in September 2026 and launched an AI CFO agent, Ari; runway.com was sold to an unrelated video-AI company [unverified, mostly press and one profile].

## 1. Top tools by stage

**Banking and cards (business checking, corporate cards, spend management)**
- Idea / pre-revenue: Mercury (free checking, $0/mo; Plus $29.90/mo, Pro $299/mo) [mercury.com/pricing, fetched 2026-10-14]. Relay (free, checking earns 0% APY, savings tiers) [unverified]. Novo (free, for solo founders and freelancers; no 2026 pricing found) [unverified].
- First customers: Mercury, Ramp Free (expense management, approvals, AP tools at $0 software cost; Plus $15/user/mo, annual about $12) [corpay.com/resources/blog/ramp-pricing, Sep 2026, secondary]. Brex Card still used for high-limit cards and travel ($25 per booked trip) [unverified].
- Scaling: Ramp is the default for spend and AP automation. Ramp reported a $44B valuation (Series F, June 2026) and over 70,000 enterprise customers [crowdfundinsider.com, June 2026; Ramp release]. Brex was acquired by Capital One for $5.15B (announced January 22, 2026; completed April 7, 2026) [capitalone.com/newsroom, Apr 2026]. Brex still runs as a Capital One unit under founder Pedro Franceschi.

**Payments and billing**
- Idea / pre-revenue: Stripe Checkout plus Payment Links. Standard card rate 2.9% + 30c [stripe.com/billing/pricing, fetched].
- First customers: Stripe Billing, pay-as-you-go at 0.7% of Billing volume, with no monthly fee; "Pay monthly" tiers from $620/mo (up to $100K volume) to $5,750/mo (up to $1M) [stripe.com/billing/pricing, fetched 2026-10-14]. Stripe Tax at 0.5% per transaction in jurisdictions where you are registered (no-code integrations) [dodopayments.com/blogs/stripe-tax-explained, secondary].
- Merchant of record (MoR): Paddle (flat 5% + 50c, no monthly fee, review-site figure) [ecommerce-platforms.com review, secondary]. Lemon Squeezy (5% + 50c, plus 1.5% for international cards) [rework.com, secondary]. Lemon Squeezy was acquired by Stripe in 2024 and is being steered toward Stripe Managed Payments [pymnts.com, 2024]. Managed Payments: a reported 3.5% surcharge on top of standard processing [paynow.gg, secondary; Dodo Payments puts all-in US near 6.4% + 30c, unverified]. Polar: official docs list 4% + 40c with extra surcharges (+1.5% non-US cards, +0.5% subscriptions) [docs.polar.sh fees]. A June 2026 review says Polar raised its base to 5% + 50c in May 2026 [fungies.io, Jun 22 2026, unverified].

**Subscription analytics**
- Idea / pre-revenue: Stripe Dashboard MRR (free) and ChartMogul free tier (up to about $120K ARR) [baremetrics.com, g2.com; secondary].
- Scaling: ChartMogul (paid by MRR band), Baremetrics (about $75–$129/mo entry, varies by source). ProfitWell is now part of Paddle; it is free for basic metrics and takes a transaction fee on some plans [secondary]. Monetizely: no 2026 pricing found.

- First customers: Xero Growing (US $55/mo now, $59 after Oct 1, 2026 per one source); QuickBooks Plus $115/mo, Advanced $275/mo (one source says $340) [beancount.io, Aug 2026; sdocpa.com, Sep 2026; secondary, conflicting]. Gusto for payroll.

- Scaling / AI-native: Digits (aimed at accounting firms; Agentic Close, MCP server launched April 2026; payroll integrations expanded to 18 providers; about $97.5M raised per CB Insights) [cpapracticeadvisor.com, June 2026; CB Insights]. - Bench's customers went to Kick (recommended in closure notice), to Employer.com's continuing service, or to QuickBooks/Xero plus a bookkeeper [techcrunch.com, Dec 27 2024; siliconangle.com, Dec 30 2024].

## 2. Core workflows (weekly, by user story)

1. **Reconcile the card feed.** Open the card transactions list, match each to a receipt, and assign a GL category and department. UI: transaction table, receipt slot, category dropdown, "missing receipt" flag. Data: card transactions, merchant, amount, GL account.
2. **Approve spend requests.** Review a pending purchase request (vendor, amount, reason), approve or reject, optionally set a budget. UI: approval queue with policy violation badges. Data: requests, budgets, policy rules.
3. **Review the AI bookkeeper's categorizations.** Accept or edit suggested categories for uncategorized transactions; confirm the rule now applies to future ones. UI: suggested-category chip, bulk accept, "why" tooltip. Data: transactions, rules, confidence.
4. **Run the monthly close checklist.** Lock the month: reconcile bank and card, post accruals, review deferred revenue, check P&L against last month. UI: checklist with status, variance column, sign-off. Data: GL, bank feeds, prior-period balances.
5. **Check failed and disputed payments.** Open the failed-payment list in Stripe or Paddle, see retry status, send dunning, refund or re-collect. UI: payments table with status filter, invoice detail, retry schedule. Data: charges, invoices, decline codes.
6. **Review MRR movements.** Check new, expansion, contraction, churn and reactivation in the analytics dashboard; click a churned customer to see the cause. UI: waterfall chart, customer table, cohort view. Data: subscriptions, invoices, plan changes.
7. **Pay vendor bills.** Upload or forward an invoice, match to a PO or vendor, set approval, schedule ACH or check. UI: inbox of bills, approval chain, payment run. Data: vendors, bills, bank accounts.
8. **Calculate sales tax for a new market.** Enter customer location; confirm tax rate, registration status, and filing due date per state or country. UI: jurisdiction table, filing calendar, exemption certificates. Data: transactions by jurisdiction, thresholds.
9. **Update runway.** Enter cash balance, burn, and hiring plan; read months remaining and the cash-out date; test a scenario. UI: runway number, burn chart, scenario toggle. Data: bank balance, monthly burn, revenue.
10. **Sweep idle cash.** Check the operating balance against the target; move excess to a treasury or savings account; read the APY. UI: account balances, transfer, yield rate. Data: account balances, sweep rules.

## 3. Must-have features for a clone

- **Banking:** multi-account view (operating, tax reserve, treasury), ACH and wire transfers, card issuance with per-card limits, transaction list with receipts and memos, approval workflows.
- **Payments:** charge and subscription list, failed payment queue with dunning and retries, refund flow, invoice generation, tax line on invoices, MoR fee shown separately from processor fee.
- **Analytics:** MRR waterfall (new, expansion, contraction, churn, reactivation), customer table with plan and LTV, cohort retention grid, net revenue retention, churn reasons.
- **Bookkeeping:** chart of accounts, bank feed reconciliation, categorization queue with AI suggestions, month-end close checklist, P&L and balance sheet, accrual toggle.
- **Tax:** jurisdiction and nexus table, rate lookup, filing calendar, exemption certificates.
- **FP&A:** runway card (cash, burn, months left, cash-out date), three-statement or simple forecast, scenarios, hiring plan toggle.
- **Bill pay:** bill inbox, approval chain, vendor list, payment run.
- **Key metrics and reports:** cash balance, net burn, runway months, MRR, gross margin (after payment fees), churn, DSO, AR aging.

Integrations to show: Stripe, QuickBooks/Xero, Gusto, Brex/Ramp/Mercury cards, Slack for approvals.

## 4. What changed with AI in 2025–2026

- **Automated now:** transaction categorization at high accuracy on routine items; receipt matching; AI close agents (Digits "Agentic Close"); MCP servers that let AI assistants query books (Digits, April 2026); FP&A agents (cfo.ai's Ari, September 2026).
- **Still needs a human:** messy source data (contracts, invoices, multi-entity), revenue recognition edge cases, tax positions, approvals over policy limits, final close sign-off. Accuracy at close is the real product test, not bank-feed pulls.
- **Failure modes:** miscategorized transactions that compound into a wrong P&L; duplicate or missed accruals; sales-tax thresholds crossed without a registration; AI forecasts that assume the same burn; low-quality receipts.
- **Market shift:** Bench's collapse showed that human-heavy bookkeeping subscriptions lose to AI-first tools (Kick, Pilot AI tier, Digits). Price cuts for AI-only tiers ($99/mo) [unverified].
- **Also:** Ramp's "token spend" management (April 2026 early access) shows spend tools now track AI API costs as a category [ramp.com, unverified].

## 5. Common mistakes small founders make

- **Over-tooling:** Brex plus Ramp plus Mercury plus Pilot plus Stripe Tax before the first $10K MRR. Pick one card, one bank, one ledger [design].
- **Enterprise plans early:** Bill.com-class AP or Ramp Plus platform fees before there is a monthly approval workload [design].
- **Leaving sales tax to later:** Crossing thresholds (state-by-state or EU VAT) without registration causes back-tax bills. Check Stripe Tax or Anrok at the first non-US sale [design].
- **Choosing an MoR without reading the fee math:** Paddle and Lemon Squeezy at 5% + 50c cost much more than Stripe at 2.9% + 30c on high-ticket sales; Managed Payments adds a surcharge on top of standard fees [design; secondary sources].
- **Relying on a single bookkeeper or AI tool with no close checklist:** Bench customers lost access to tax documents when the service closed. Export your books monthly [TechCrunch, Dec 2024].
- **Using pre-close numbers for runway:** Runway is only as good as the bank balance and burn you enter. Use a bank-linked number, not a spreadsheet guess [design].
- **Ignoring vendor-change risk:** Causal, Pry and Runway all changed owners or names; keep models in exportable form [design].
