# Segment group 2: trades, general contractors, trucking

Research date: 2026-10-14. Marking: [design] = my estimate, not a sourced number. Where a sourced number exists I give URL and date. Several segments have thin public data on sales cycles, procurement and churn, so most of those figures are [design]. Vendor-authored surveys are flagged.

---

## Segment 1: Trades and specialty subcontractors (roofing, HVAC, plumbing, electrical; 2-50 employees)

### 1. Size & examples
- Owner-led residential and light-commercial service firms: roofing, HVAC, plumbing, electrical. Typical 2-50 employees, $300K-$10M revenue [design]. Many are under 10 staff.
- Sample caveat: ServiceTitan's 2025 Exterior Trades survey covered 1,020 owners and executives with over $1M revenue, fielded Jan 3-22, 2025 (https://get.servicetitan.com/rs/304-EAE-038/images/0102038-ST-ExteriorsReport-2025v3.pdf). That is above this segment's size band.
- Examples: a 6-truck residential HVAC shop, a 12-person roofing crew with two sales reps, a 3-van plumbing company, a 20-person electrical sub.

### 2. Buying committee
| Role | Typical titles | Count | Influence (0-1) | Main concerns | Can block? | Present |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Economic buyer / signer | Owner, president, co-owner | 1 | 0.9 | Cash flow, ticket size, "will this pay for itself" | Yes | Always |
| Champion / main user | Office manager, dispatcher, service coordinator | 1-2 | 0.5 | Scheduling, invoicing, less double entry | Yes (quietly) | Always |
| Field user | Techs, estimators, salespeople | 2-15 | 0.2 | Phone app friction, extra clicks | Yes, via non-use | Often |
| Finance | Outside bookkeeper or CPA | 0-1 | 0.4 | QuickBooks sync, reconciling | Yes | Sometimes |
| Spouse / family partner | Co-owner, spouse who runs books | 0-1 | 0.3 [design] | Monthly cost, risk | Yes, in family firms | Sometimes |
| PE rollup corporate (growing share) | Platform CFO, ops VP | 0-1 | 0.7 [design] | Standard stack, security | Yes | Only in acquired firms |
| IT / security / legal | None usually | 0 | under 0.1 | Contract signing only | Rarely | Rare |

- Buyer is not the user in roughly 60-70% of deals [design]: owner buys, office manager and techs use.
- Supporting evidence that adoption is low: a TechCrunch piece on Airship (Sep 30, 2024, https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/30/airship-wants-to-build-better-sales-tools-for-hvac-contractors) says many HVAC owners still use pen and paper and that PE rollups are consolidating the trades.

### 3. Cycle & deal size
- Typical ACV for a small vendor: $600-$5K/yr [design]. Costbench reports median annual cost of $699 for Jobber (46 deals) and $4,400 for Housecall Pro (11 deals), with small samples (https://www.costbench.com/compare/housecall-pro-vs-jobber/, accessed 2026).
- Cycle, days [design; no sourced trades cycle data found]:
  - Under $5K: 1-21 days, often a card purchase in one call.
  - $5-25K: 14-45 days.
  - $25-100K (multi-location, PE rollup): 45-120 days.
  - Over $100K: 90-180 days.
- Win rate on qualified opportunities: 15-30% [design].

### 4. Procurement
- Usually a company credit card or ACH, monthly plans. Formal procurement is rare under 50 staff. Card limits are not published; treat as under $5K/purchase [design].
- Quotes and RFPs: none below the owner's comfort level. Owners buy on a demo plus a price.
- Payment terms: card or ACH monthly; annual prepay is accepted by some [design].
- Paperwork: W-9 only when a payer or PE platform sets up a vendor master. COI and insurance rarely requested. Security questionnaire, SOC 2, VPAT, BAA, DPA: not required under 50 staff, but a PE platform will ask for them [design].
- Budget year: most are calendar-year; budgets set in Q4 by owner [design].

### 5. Channels
- Works: peer referral in local trade groups and Facebook groups; integration marketplaces (QuickBooks, Housecall Pro/Jobber/ServiceTitan ecosystems) [design]; in-person demo at the shop; phone follow-up; trade association events (PHCP, ACCA, state contractor groups) [design].
- Fails: cold email with generic copy; LinkedIn for owners of firms under 20 staff [design]; big booths with no offer.
- Best times to reach: early morning before dispatch or late evening; avoid peak demand days [design].
- Seasonality of demand (affects reachability): HVAC search demand swings by 250-600% peak to valley; plumbing rises in mid-summer and winter; roofing is steady (WebFX via Stacker, Feb 19, 2026, https://krdo.com/stacker-small-business/2026/02/19/seasonal-search-shifts-in-home-services-demand-what-spikes-when-and-how-to-stay-booked-year-round/).
- Tech-savviness: low to mixed. NFPA 2025 trade survey: 44% cite cost of implementation as the main barrier to new tech (https://fireandsafetyjournalamericas.com/skilled-labor-shortage-and-technology-adoption-highlighted-in-nfpas-2025-industry-trends-survey/, 2025; adjacent trades, not HVAC).

### 6. Methodology
- Best fit: value selling with a light MEDDIC (economic buyer = owner, champion = office manager). Full MEDDICC is too heavy for a 10-person shop [design].
- Critical elements: (1) the owner's dollar pain (missed calls, slow invoicing, ticket size); (2) the champion identified in the first call; (3) a 20-minute owner demo tied to dollars per month; (4) the QuickBooks or bookkeeper check.
- Airship claims an average 20% ticket-size increase for its users (vendor claim, TechCrunch Sep 30, 2024), which is a useful framing but unverified.

### 7. Scenarios
1. Office manager loves it, owner says no (buyer is not user), p about 0.35 [design]. Handle: ask for a 20-minute owner session; show the cost against one saved lost call per month; offer a month-to-month trial.
2. Champion leaves or is dropped in a slow season, p about 0.10-0.15. Handle: multi-thread to the owner early; hold a second champion (lead tech or bookkeeper).
3. Bookkeeper blocks on QuickBooks sync, p about 0.15. Handle: test the sync in the first demo; ask the bookkeeper in writing before the trial ends.
4. Bottom-up: techs use a free tier, owner must convert, p about 0.20 [design]. Handle: convert with a usage report and a single-price plan.
5. PE rollup buys the firm mid-deal, p about 0.10 [design]. Handle: expect a procurement form and a security questionnaire; have a SOC 2 status page and a standard DPA.
6. Seasonal stall (summer HVAC rush, storm season for roofing), p high. Handle: book demos in shoulder months; offer a 60-day start.

### 8. Common objections
- "We already use ServiceTitan / Jobber / Housecall Pro." Reply with a migration or add-on check, not a replacement pitch.
- "I'm too busy to learn another system." Reply: "Set it up in one afternoon with your office manager; I'll do the import."
- "I can't afford another monthly fee." Reply: show payback in one job per month.
- "My guys won't use it." Reply: "Who on the team will push it? Let's pilot with the two most open techs."
- "QuickBooks won't sync." Reply: demo the sync live; name the bookkeeper's role.
- "The last software was a waste." Reply: ask what failed; offer a 30-day exit clause.

### 9. Churn & seasonality
- Annual logo churn for SMB field-service software: 25-40% [design]. Monthly plans churn faster than annual prepay.
- Renewal dynamics: churn spikes after a slow season when cash is tight; owners cut tools first.
- Busy months for sales: January-March (budget set) and September-October (owners plan the next year) [design]. Slow months: June-August for HVAC owners (busy in service, no time for buying) and mid-December [design]. An HVAC owner's column (2023) says summer is busy and tapers in August; an Acctsage release (Apr 24, 2026, https://www.mannpublications.com/mannreport/2026/04/24/boynton-beach-accounting-firm-reveals-why-most-hvac-businesses-lose-profit-during-their-busiest-season/) reports that HVAC firms often show strong revenue but weak cash flow in fall and winter (anecdotal).

### 10. Solo-founder fit
- 0.6 [design]. A solo founder can sell a sub-$5K product by phone and demo, and can support a 2-50 person shop. Field integrations and seasonal support are heavy. Works best with a narrow wedge (one trade, one integration).

## Segment 2: General contractors and mid-size construction firms (50-1,000 employees)

### 1. Size & examples
- Regional and mid-market GCs and specialty contractors, 50-1,000 employees, roughly $25M-$1B revenue [design]. Job-size band often cited as $50M-$150M projects for mid-market (startup.jobs listing, ALICE Technologies, undated; https://startup.jobs/account-executive-mid-market-alice-technologies-8219580).
- Examples: a regional commercial GC with 200 staff and 30 active jobs; a 400-person mechanical contractor; a 600-person civil/site contractor.
- Market context: Dodge expects total starts to rise 4% to $1.26T in 2026 (ULI, citing Dodge, https://urbanland.uli.org/capital-markets-and-finance/economist-snapshot-forecasting-construction-costs-outlook-for-2026, undated 2026). An AGC digest (Sep 25, 2026, https://www.agc.org/sites/default/files/users/user21902/datadigest20260925_.pdf) reports ConstructConnect starts up 12% YTD through August, with data centers and offices driving nonresidential and residential falling. A Dodge/CMiC study (May 4, 2026, https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260504513164/en/New-Study-72-of-Contractors-Expect-Negative-Impacts-from-Policy-and-Cost-Pressures-Yet-Optimism-Holds-for-2026) finds 72% expect negative impacts from policy and cost pressure.

### 2. Buying committee
| Role | Typical titles | Count | Influence | Main concerns | Can block? | Present |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Economic buyer | CEO, president, COO | 1 | 0.7 | Margin, job-cost control, rework | Yes | Late stage |
| Champion | VP operations, VP preconstruction, director of IT/ops | 1-2 | 0.7 | Fixes a known project pain | Yes | Always |
| Users | Project managers, superintendents, estimators | 5-50+ | 0.3 | Field use, extra paperwork | Yes, via non-adoption | Often |
| Finance | CFO, controller | 1-2 | 0.6 | Job-cost integration, payment terms | Yes | Late stage |
| IT / security | IT director, security analyst | 1 | 0.5 | SSO, data, integrations, SOC 2 | Yes | Late stage, always gates |
| Legal / contracts | GC counsel, contracts manager | 1 | 0.3 | MSA, liability, data terms | Yes, on terms | Late stage |
| Safety | Safety director | 0-1 | 0.4 | Field-facing tools, incident data | Sometimes | Mid |
| Risk / insurance | Insurance or risk manager | 0-1 | 0.3 | Vendor insurance (COI) | Rarely | Late |
| Purchasing | Procurement manager | 0-1 | 0.3 (higher above 500 staff) | Vendor onboarding, PO | Yes, on process | Late |
| Union | Business agent | 0 | under 0.1 | Rarely relevant | Rarely | Rare |

- Buyer is not the user in roughly 70-80% of deals [design].

### 3. Cycle & deal size
- Construction SaaS averages 147 days to close vs 67 days for general SaaS (saashero, Sep 3, 2026, https://saashero.net/strategy/construction-saas-pipeline-acceleration/; secondary consultant claim, unverified).
- Agency market table: $25K-$300K ACV and 60-180 day cycles (https://www.devcommx.com/fractional-revops/mesa/construction-tech; looks like one repeated template, use only as directional).
- Cycle by ACV, days [design]:
  - Under $5K: 14-45.
  - $5-25K: 30-90.
  - $25-100K: 90-180.
  - Over $100K: 150-270.
- Typical small-vendor ACV: $10K-$60K [design]. For scale: ServiceTitan had 9,500 active customers at Jan 31, 2025 (press release, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1638826/000095017025048834/ttan-20250131.htm); third-party estimates of about $78K revenue per active customer (Sacra) are not company disclosures.
- Win rate on qualified: 15-25% [design].

### 4. Procurement
- Purchase orders and vendor master setup common. Net-30 to net-60 standard [design]. P-card limits typically $2.5K-$10K [design].
- Thresholds: RFP or three-quote rules at the firm's own level, often $25K-$100K [design]. Board approval rare below $250K [design].
- Fiscal year: calendar or April/July start; budgets set Q4 [design].
- Paperwork: W-9 (vendor master), COI with GL $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate, auto $1M, umbrella $1M-$5M, A.M. Best A- (Alkeme broker guide, Apr 2026, https://construction.aca.alkemeins.com/construction/resources/subcontractor-insurance/; PARIC 2025 prequalification form, https://paric.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/2025-Subcontractor_Supplier-Prequalification.pdf). Those insurance rules apply to subcontractors; for software vendors, a COI and W-9 are common asks [design].
- Security questionnaire (SIG, CAIQ), SOC 2 Type II, DPA, and vendor prequal portal: expect from any firm above 200 staff [design].

### 5. Channels
- Works: founder-led sales to VP operations and preconstruction; reference customers and peer referral; integrations with project-management platforms; pilots on a single project. Practical Founders (ep. 48, https://practicalfounders.com/podcast/48-built-grew-sold-and-kept-growing-a-construction-software-company-sam-knight/) and FounderPath's Document Crunch profile (https://founderpath.com/resources/josh-levy-document-crunch-construction-contract-software-series-a) both describe founder-led sales to large contractors' legal or ops teams first.
- Fails: cold outreach to CEOs with generic pitches; trade-show booths without a pilot offer; reseller-only plans without a named champion [design].
- Best times: Q4 and January-March (budget and planning season) [design]; avoid the first two weeks of December.
- Tech-savviness: office staff moderate; field staff low. Adoption is historically slow (qualitative, per agency page above).

### 6. Methodology
- Best fit: MEDDPICC with a mutual action plan. Long, multi-threaded deals need the economic buyer, champion, paper process and decision criteria (security and integrations) mapped early [design].
- Critical elements: (1) a dollar metric (rework, change orders, job-cost variance); (2) two champions; (3) early security pre-read; (4) a written decision process with dates; (5) a pilot with success criteria.

### 7. Scenarios
1. Security questionnaire stalls late, p about 0.35 [design]. Handle: prepare a standard security pack, SOC 2 status, and DPA before the first demo.
2. Champion (VP ops) leaves mid-cycle, p about 0.15. Handle: keep a second champion from the project team; re-run the business case with the new owner.
3. Pilot on one project works but expansion stalls, p about 0.30. Handle: agree a success plan with the CFO and a multi-project rollout date.
4. Forced RFP above the company threshold, p about 0.20 [design]. Handle: answer the RFP once in a reusable template; push for scoring criteria that favor your strengths.
5. Bottom-up: field PMs use a free tier; GC wants enterprise terms, p about 0.20. Handle: convert with usage data and an admin-level plan.
6. Budget freeze after a starts slowdown, p about 0.15 [design]. Handle: offer a smaller initial scope or a deferred start.

### 8. Common objections
- "Procore already does this." Reply: name the one workflow you do better and offer to integrate.
- "Our PMs will never use it." Reply: pilot with the two most open PMs and report usage weekly.
- "Send us your SOC 2 report." Reply: send the status page and a bridge letter.
- "We need a three-year price lock." Reply: offer a two-year lock with a cap.
- "Let's revisit next quarter." Reply: ask for a date and what must be true by then.

### 9. Churn & seasonality
- Annual logo churn for mid-market construction software: 8-15% [design]. Renewals cluster on the anniversary of the contract, so track renewal dates per account.
- Busy: Q4 and January-March. Slow: summer in northern regions and the mid-December holidays [design].
- Market risk: slower new business is now a bigger concern than labor for many contractors (Dodge/CMiC, May 2026, see Size section), which can freeze budgets.

### 10. Solo-founder fit
- 0.25 [design]. Long cycles, security reviews and multiple stakeholders make it hard for one person. Feasible only with a narrow wedge, a reseller or integration partner, and a pre-built security pack.

## Segment 3: Logistics and trucking fleets (owner-operators to mid-size carriers)

### 1. Size & examples
- Owner-operators (1 truck, often the owner drives), small carriers (2-20 trucks), mid-size carriers (50-500+ trucks) [design for bands].
- ATA, American Trucking Trends 2025 (covering 2024): 91.5% of carriers ran 10 or fewer trucks; 99.3% ran under 100 power units; industry revenue $906B in 2024, down from $1.004T in 2023; 3.58M professional drivers (https://www.truckersnews.com/news/article/15754113/briefly-atas-american-trucking-trends-report-now-available, 2025).
- OOIDA reports more than 150,000 members (Land Line, https://landline.media/?p=41993, undated; OOIDA ADAS survey context).
- Examples: a single-truck flatbed operator; a 6-truck reefer carrier with a dispatcher; a 40-truck dry van fleet with a safety manager.

### 2. Buying committee
| Role | Typical titles | Count | Influence | Main concerns | Can block? | Present |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Economic buyer / user | Owner-operator, owner | 1 | 0.9-1.0 for owner-ops | Cash per week, compliance time | Yes | Always |
| Dispatcher / ops | Dispatcher, ops manager | 1-3 | 0.5 | Load tracking, sharing with drivers | Yes | Small and mid fleets |
| Safety / compliance | Safety manager, DQ-file clerk | 0-1 | 0.5 | DOT, ELD, drug testing, audits | Yes | Mid fleets |
| Driver | Company or owner-op driver | 2-100+ | 0.2 | App friction, pay | Yes, by refusing use | Always in fleets |
| Finance | Bookkeeper, controller, CFO | 0-1 | 0.4 | Payment, factoring, IFTA, tolls | Yes | Mid fleets |
| Factoring company | Factor's onboarding team | 0-1 | 0.4 | Payee name, EIN, approvals | Yes, on payment flow | Some carriers |
| IT / security | IT director | 0-1 | 0.3 | Data and integrations | Yes | Rare (mid-size only) |
| Customer / shipper / broker | Their vendor team | 0-1 | 0.5 when it applies | Their portal and carrier requirements | Yes | Varies |

- Buyer is not the user in about 0% of owner-operator deals, 40-60% of small fleets, and 70%+ of mid-size [design].

### 3. Cycle & deal size
- Owner-operators: same day to 14 days, card purchase [design].
- Small fleets (2-20 trucks), under $5K: 7-30 days; $5-25K: 14-60 days [design].
- Mid-size, over $25K: 60-150 days [design].
- Typical ACV [design]: owner-op $100-$300/yr; 2-20 trucks $1K-$6K/yr; mid-size $10K-$100K/yr. Trucker Path Command lists from $99/month (vendor pricing, undated).
- Budget context: a 2020-era RXO survey of 255 carriers (85 owner-operators) says small fleets have no unlimited budget and each purchase needs a clear payoff (https://rxo.com/?p=15739, 2020; dated).
- Win rate on qualified: 20-35% for small fleets [design]; 10-20% for mid-size [design].

### 4. Procurement
- Owner-ops: card, no procurement.
- Small fleets: card or ACH, monthly.
- Mid-size and broker-linked carriers: vendor onboarding with W-9 (legal name and EIN must match FMCSA; mismatched payee names are a common payment-fraud vector, per a vendor blog, https://loadconnect.io/blog/carrier-vetting-checklist-10-checks-brokers-should-make, undated), COI with auto liability $1M and cargo $100K (broker minimums, same source), a signed broker-carrier or service agreement, and FMCSA authority check.
- Security questionnaire, SOC 2 Type II and DPA: expected when the product handles driver PII or integrates with a shipper or broker [design].
- Fiscal year: calendar; budgets often set in Q4 or January [design].
- Payment terms: net-30 standard for carriers paid by brokers; owners often pay monthly [design].

### 5. Channels
- Works: owner-operator communities (Facebook groups, trucking YouTube and podcasts, truck-stop and driver forums) [design]; integrations with factoring companies, load boards and ELD/TMS products [design]; peer referral through dispatch services; direct outreach to owners and VPs of small carriers on LinkedIn (a 2025-era job posting for a trucking software company describes booking demos with owners and ops managers this way, undated).
- Fails: enterprise-style demos to owner-operators; sponsored "best TMS" comparison pages as a sole channel [design]; paid search for single-truck owners [design].
- Best times: slower freight months (January-February) when owners have time to evaluate [design]; avoid Q4 peak freight and holiday season.
- Tech-savviness: phone-first for owner-ops. Tech.co 2025 logistics report: 48% of owner-operators say they changed their business model for new technology vs 63% of logistics managers (https://tech.co/article/logistics/logistics-report-2025, 2025). A FreightWaves piece on trucker tech adoption says drivers adopt tech "only if it helps earnings" (https://www.freightwaves.com/?p=213520, undated).

### 6. Methodology
- Owner-operators and 2-20 truck fleets: value selling with a simple dollar ROI (hours saved per week times rate). Sandler-style pain-first questions fit an owner who buys fast [design].
- Mid-size: MEDDIC-lite, with the safety and finance stakeholders mapped in the first call. Critical elements: the compliance pain in dollars, the dispatcher or safety champion, and the factoring or payment path.

### 7. Scenarios
1. Owner-op buys, then churns after one compliance issue is resolved elsewhere, p about 0.20 [design]. Handle: put the tool in the compliance calendar, not only the dispatch flow.
2. Mid-size safety director blocks on DQ-file or ELD integration, p about 0.25 [design]. Handle: map the integration in the first call; provide a sandbox.
3. Driver app adoption fails in a small fleet, p about 0.30 [design]. Handle: pilot with two drivers and a bonus or pay-linked feature.
4. Factoring company or payee-name mismatch stalls onboarding, p about 0.15 [design]. Handle: collect W-9 and factor letter early; match legal names.
5. Shipper or broker requires use of its portal, p about 0.15 [design]. Handle: check the requirement before the demo; ask whether the portal replaces or adds to your tool.
6. Freight downturn cuts budgets, p about 0.20 [design]. ATA shows revenue down 10% in 2024 (truckersnews, 2025). Handle: offer monthly pricing and a per-truck tier that scales down.

### 8. Common objections
- "I'm just one truck, I don't need software." Reply: show the 10 hours per month of compliance work (Fleetworthy, below) and ask what it would be worth to recover them.
- "Excel and QuickBooks are fine." Reply: ask how long month-end takes now.
- "Who pays for the driver app?" Reply: price per truck, not per driver, with a free driver tier [design].
- "My last TMS was a waste." Reply: ask what failed; offer a 30-day exit clause. A FleetOwner case study describes a 17-truck carrier that replaced a small-fleet TMS with a dispatch-first product (https://www.fleetowner.com/operations/article/21278009/small-fleet-finds-room-to-grow-with-adaptive-tms-software).
- "I'll pay per load, not per month." Reply: offer a per-load option if the load volume is stable.

### 9. Churn & seasonality
- Annual churn [design]: owner-operators 30-50%; small fleets 20-30%; mid-size 10-20%. Owner-ops churn when freight drops or when they leave the market.
- Fleetworthy's 2025 survey (Jan 2025; 300 respondents including owner-operators): 35% of owner-operators say they have considered ceasing operations because of compliance workload; 22% spend more than 10 hours a month on compliance; the infographic and press release disagree on some figures (https://fleetworthy.com/?p=7333 and https://fleetworthy.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/2025-Trucking-Insights-Infographic-Owner-Operators.pdf). Fleetworthy is a vendor, so treat as self-reported.
- Adoption gap (old data): InMotion Global 2018 survey: only about a third of carriers with fewer than 10 trucks used a TMS; single-truck operators at 7%; about 91% of carriers with 20+ trucks used one. No direct URL retrieved; treat as dated.
- Busy: freight peak in Q4 and holiday; slow: January-February [design]. Monthly tonnage data comes from the ATA Truck Tonnage Index.

### 10. Solo-founder fit
- Owner-operators: 0.8 [design]. Self-serve and phone sales work; one person can support it.
- Small fleets (2-20 trucks): 0.6 [design]. Feasible with a clean onboarding and a dispatch-first wedge.
- Mid-size: 0.15 [design]. Security, integrations and long cycles need a team.
