# Health segments: buyer research (late 2026)

Conventions: [design] = my estimate, not sourced. [unverified] = only found in vendor or secondary blogs. Search coverage for 2025–2026 buying-process data was thin; most process numbers below are design estimates, and the sources list says which numbers have real backing.

---

## Segment 1: Independent dental, medical and veterinary practices (incl. DSO-owned vs independent)

**1. Size & examples**
- Typical: 1–10 dentists or vets; 3–30 staff; $0.5M–$5M revenue per site. Solo and 2-doctor practices are the largest group.
- Examples: a solo general dentist in a strip mall; a 3-vet small-animal clinic; a 2-physician family medicine group; a DSO-owned site (e.g., a 20-location dental group with central purchasing).
- Ownership: 72.5% of US dentists were practice owners in 2023, down from 84.7% in 2005 (ADA HPI, [ada.org dental-practice-research](https://www.ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/dental-practice-research), accessed 2026-10). DSO affiliation was 16% of dentists in 2024 (same source; 7.2% in 2015), and 23–26% among dentists within 10 years of graduation. Vets: JAVMA says corporate groups held about half of US domestic vet revenue by 2023 (via [Biltmore Beacon, 2025](https://www.biltmorebeacon.com/news/private-equity-is-reshaping-asheville-s-vet-clinics-they-re-circling-me-like-a-vulture/article_b3429631-57fe-45dc-a65b-747d665cc547.html)). Clinic-count estimates run 30–50% corporate, which is not a formal survey ([Patch, undated](https://patch.com/california/agourahills/s/kk1q4/corporate-owners-quietly-reshape-veterinary-care-across-ca-is-your-vet-one-of-them)).
- DSO vs independent: DSOs buy centrally (IT, procurement, a VP of operations or CFO), with standard tech stacks and a multi-site contract. Independents buy with the owner and office manager, one site at a time. Plan separate motions for each. Independents are the easier first sale; DSOs are slower but bigger.

**2. Buying committee** (counts are [design] unless noted)

| Role | Typical titles | Count | Influence | Main concern | Can block? | Presence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| User | hygienist, front desk, tech, vet tech, MA | 3–15 | 0.3 | Speed, workflow change | Soft (refuses adoption) | Daily |
| Office manager | office manager, practice administrator | 1 | 0.7 | Time to implement, staff training | Yes, often runs the evaluation | Daily |
| Champion / economic buyer | owner dentist/doctor/vet | 1 | 0.9 | Cost, lost revenue during switch | Yes | Weekly |
| Spouse/partner | owner's spouse (often finance) | 0–1 | 0.3 | Cash, risk | Rarely, but can veto | Occasional |
| IT / technical | outside IT vendor (MSP) | 0–1 | 0.4 | Integrations, backups, hardware | Yes, on integration | Rare |
| Finance/billing | billing coordinator, outside bookkeeper or CPA | 0–1 | 0.3 | Payments, reconciliation | Rarely | Occasional |
| Security/legal | none, usually | 0 | 0.1 | HIPAA paperwork | Rare | Rare |
| DSO HQ (DSO only) | VP ops, CFO, procurement, IT director | 3–6 | 0.8–1.0 | Standardization, cost | Yes | Scheduled meetings |

- Buyer is not the user in roughly 60–70% of deals [design].
- Dental: the owner signs but the office manager often runs the evaluation (anecdotal, [dentaleconomics, 2003](https://dentaleconomics.com/practice/systems/article/16387920/making-software-buying-decisions)).
- Vet: owners and practice managers buy, but vets can block through use. Switching is feared. Some practices stay on legacy systems because of data-migration problems (VIN News, Sep 2024 (via search, no direct URL retrieved), via [dvm360](https://www.dvm360.com/view/switching-your-veterinary-practice-software)).
- Medical: physician owners and practice administrators; EHR decisions are often tied to a PE-backed MSO.

**3. Cycle & deal size** [design unless noted]
- <$5k (per-seat tools, scheduling, reminders): 1–14 days; one decision-maker, often a card.
- $5–25k/yr (single-site PMS add-ons, billing): 14–45 days.
- $25–100k/yr (multi-site or DSO pilot): 60–150 days.
- >$100k/yr (DSO enterprise): 4–9 months.
- Typical ACV a small vendor gets: $1.5k–$12k for independents; $30k–$150k for a DSO contract [design].
- Win rate on qualified opportunities: 20–30% for independents with a demo and trial, 10–20% for DSOs [design]. Bridge Group reported 19% median SaaS win rate in 2024, down from 23% in 2022 ([via Everstage](https://www.everstage.com/sales-compensation/saas-sales-compensation-benchmarks), [unverified secondary]).

**4. Procurement**
- Card: P-card or credit card, $1k–$5k per transaction. Most independents pay by card; invoice/ACH by annual plan.
- Quotes: none needed for independents. DSOs require 3-quote or procurement sign-off above $10k–$25k [design].
- Board approval: none for independents; DSO capex/opex committee above ~$50k [design].
- Fiscal year: calendar year for most practices; DSO fiscal years vary. Budgets usually set in Q4 for the next year. Q4 is the best time to sell annual plans.
- Payment terms: card or net 15–30 for DSOs [design].
- Paperwork: W-9 (usually yes); certificate of insurance (cyber and E&O, $1M–$2M) for DSOs; a BAA for any PHI (HIPAA applies to dental and medical; vet does not, so skip the BAA there); security questionnaire (DSO only); SOC 2 not required under $25k; VPAT not relevant.
- HIPAA: the 2025 Security Rule update is still a proposal. Comment period closed 2025-03-07. Current target is reportedly July 2027 ([vendor article, Jul 2026, unverified](https://konfirmity.com/blog/hipaa-what-changed-in-2026)).

**5. Channels**
- Works: in-person demos at practice, peer referral from a same-type practice, state and local dental/vet society events, study clubs, integrations with the PMS (Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental, Cornerstone, AVImark, ezyVet), Henry Schein / Patterson / Benco reseller or partner programs, Facebook groups for dental office managers.
- Trade shows: Chicago Midwinter drew 21,000+ attendees in 2024 and 500+ exhibitors in 2026 ([Chicago Dental Society](https://www.cds.org/2026-midwinter-meeting/)). Expensive; work only for a strong-fit DSO pipeline.
- Fails: cold email to office managers (low response); LinkedIn to dentists; generic paid ads. Phone works best in the morning before patient hours (about 7:30–9:30 am) and after 5 pm.
- Tech-savviness: low to medium; prefers a phone call and a recorded walkthrough.

**6. Methodology**
- Independents: value selling plus a short BANT. Lead with the owner's money and time (no-shows, staff hours, collections). Pilot with one hygienist or one technician.
- DSOs: MEDDICC. Find the economic buyer (VP ops) and the IT veto. Use a 60-day pilot at 2–3 sites.

**7. Scenarios**
1. Office manager loves it, owner won't sign (P ~35%). Send an ROI sheet and offer a 30-day trial with no commitment. Ask for a 15-minute call with the owner.
2. Owner asks for a 2-week trial, then goes quiet (P ~30%). Schedule a training session before the trial ends. Ask what would have to be true to sign this week.
3. DSO pilot at one site, then the HQ stalls on procurement (P ~20%). Get the VP ops to sponsor a security questionnaire early; expect 3–6 months.
4. Practice sells to a DSO mid-deal (P ~10%). Check contract assignment; new owner usually swaps tools within 12 months.
5. Bottom-up adoption: staff use a free tier, owner doesn't pay (P ~15%). Convert with usage data; offer an annual deal in Q4.

**8. Common objections**
- "Our current software is fine." → "What did last quarter's no-show rate cost you? Could we check that together?"
- "We can't afford the downtime." → "We'll migrate on a Saturday and run both systems for a week."
- "The DSO will decide this." → "Who is your ops lead? Could we get 20 minutes with them to scope the pilot?"
- "I need to talk to my spouse." → "Happy to send a one-page summary. Could we set a call for Thursday?"

**9. Churn & seasonality**
- Annual logo churn: independents 15–25%; DSOs 8–12% [design]. Churn spikes at practice sale or retirement.
- Renewal dynamics: renewal in Q4 pairs with budgeting. Churn risk highest after software updates.
- Busy: January–March (tax and insurance season for vets/dentists is less of a factor; patient volume is high). Slow: late November to early January (holidays), July–August (vacation).

**10. Solo-founder fit: 0.7 for independents, 0.3 for DSOs.** Independents can be sold by phone and email; DSOs need an enterprise motion a 1–3 person company rarely has.

## Segment 2: Hospitals and health systems (VAC, IT security, HIPAA BAA, Epic integration)

**1. Size & examples**
- Typical: 200–5,000+ employees per hospital; health systems 5,000–100,000+ employees. Community hospitals 100–300 beds; regional systems 5–30 hospitals; AMCs 800+ beds.
- Examples: a 150-bed community hospital on Epic; a 12-hospital regional system; an academic medical center with its own IT group; a critical access hospital (25 beds).
- Financial pressure: about half of health system executives report constrained or high-risk finances, and over half hold under 180 days cash ([L.E.K. 2025 US Health System Executive Survey](https://www.lek.com/insights/medtech/state-medtech-customer-base-insights-leks-2025-us-health-system-executive-survey)). Expect cost-down scrutiny.

**2. Buying committee** (counts [design] unless noted; Gartner's general enterprise range is 5–11 stakeholders, [unverified secondary](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/6303215))

| Role | Typical titles | Count | Influence | Main concern | Can block? | Presence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| User | RN, clinician, coder, scheduler, nurse manager | 3–10 | 0.4 | Workflow, time | Yes, through adoption | Daily |
| Champion | director of ops, service-line leader, CNO | 1–2 | 0.8 | Outcome for their unit | Yes | Weekly |
| Value analysis committee (VAC) | VAC chair (physician), pharmacy, nursing, supply chain | 6–12 (committee) | 0.9 | Clinical evidence, cost, standardization | Yes; votes at scheduled meetings | Monthly |
| Economic buyer / signer | CFO, COO, CIO (for IT) | 1–2 | 1.0 | Budget, ROI | Yes | Quarterly |
| IT security | CISO, security analyst, vendor risk manager | 1–3 | 0.8 | Security questionnaire, SOC 2, pen test | Yes, hard veto | Gate only |
| Privacy officer | privacy officer, HIPAA compliance lead | 1 | 0.7 | BAA, data flows | Yes | Gate only |
| Legal | contracts counsel | 1–2 | 0.6 | Indemnity, liability cap, BAA terms | Yes | Late |
| Procurement / GPO | supply chain director, category manager, GPO rep | 1–3 | 0.7 | Contract, GPO pricing | Yes | Mid to late |
| Epic / EHR team | Epic analyst, integration engineer, application team | 1–4 | 0.7 (if integration) | Build effort, interface stability | Yes | Mid |
| Finance / capital | finance director, capital committee | 1–2 | 0.7 | Capital vs operating budget | Yes | Late |

- Buyer is not the user in nearly all deals.
- Epic is the default EHR for most large systems; integration is a gating item, not optional.

**3. Cycle & deal size** (secondary sources; ranges [design] where noted)
- Health-system sales cycles: 3–18 months (Rock Health roundup); 18–24 months consistently cited by a health-system executive (interview, [unverified]); "some deals three years" [unverified].
- <$5k: rare; pilot or department card. 1–3 months.
- $5–25k: department approval. 3–6 months, mostly security review.
- $25–100k: VAC or IT governance. 6–12 months.
- >$100k: system-wide, capital committee. 12–24 months.
- Typical small-vendor ACV: $20k–$80k per hospital; $100k+ for a system [design].
- Win rate: 15–25% for qualified hospital deals [design]. Enterprise deals over $100k ACV have a median cycle of 84 days across all B2B per Ebsta/Pavilion data ([via Knowledgelib, 2026](https://knowledgelib.io/business/sales-ops/sales-metrics-benchmarks/2026)); hospitals run longer than that.

**4. Procurement**
- Card: none for enterprise; department P-card under $2.5k–$5k [design].
- Quotes: formal RFP or 3-bid above $25k–$100k [design].
- Board approval: capital/operating committee above $100k–$250k [design]; VAC approval for clinical products.
- Fiscal year: many nonprofit systems start July 1 (FY budgets set in spring; capital planning in Q1 of calendar year) [design; a direct source was not found].
- Payment terms: net 30–60 with PO; GPO pricing overrides list.
- GPOs: Vizient and Premier are the large ones. Premier was acquired by Patient Square Capital in Sept 2025 ([Premier, 2025 news, via search, unverified]). Supplier onboarding requires documentation and sometimes fees [unverified]; I did not find current enrollment terms.
- Paperwork, all required in practice: BAA (before PHI), security questionnaire (often 150–400 questions, e.g., HECVAT-style or custom), SOC 2 Type II, pen test letter, cyber insurance COI ($5M+ at larger systems) [design], vendor registration, W-9, DPA where relevant, business continuity plan.
- HIPAA security rule update (NPRM, Jan 2025): would require BAAs to spell out MFA, encryption and annual verification of vendor controls ([vendor summary, unverified](https://www.accountablehq.com/post/hipaa-security-rule-nprm-explained-what-s-changing-and-how-to-prepare)). Comment period closed 2025-03-07; not final as of 2026-10. Plan as if it will be final.
- Epic: a Showroom listing takes 8–16 weeks of application and technical security review, and listing does not mean go-live; each hospital still runs its own review ([nirmitee blog, undated](https://nirmitee.io/blog/epic-vendor-security-review/)). Other blogs quote 3–9 months and 6–18 months for bidirectional integration. Confirm current Epic Vendor Services terms directly.

**5. Channels**
- Works: a clinician champion who sits on the VAC; a director referral from a peer system; pilot in one unit; HIMSS and HFMA conferences (expensive, only for mature products); Epic Showroom once listed; integration partnerships with Epic-certified vendors; state hospital association webinars.
- Fails: cold outreach to CIOs (low yield); free trials for the whole system; going straight to the CEO without a champion; academic medical centers early (slow, committee-heavy, per a Rock Health CEO roundup).
- Best times: Tuesday–Thursday mornings; avoid late December and early January.
- Tech-savviness: high in IT, moderate for clinicians, low for finance-side.

**6. Methodology**
- MEDDICC, with a security gate. Map the VAC calendar on day one; a missed meeting costs 4–6 weeks. Identify the economic buyer, the clinical champion and the IT security owner.
- Value selling for the CFO (capex vs opex, payback under 12 months). Use a pilot with written success criteria.

**7. Scenarios**
1. Champion leaves the hospital (P ~20%). Find a second champion within 2 weeks; re-map the VAC. Re-earn trust by sending the pilot results.
2. Security questionnaire stalls (P ~40%). Send a pre-filled SIG Lite or HECVAT-style document with SOC 2 report; name a security contact; set a 2-week response SLA.
3. VAC reviews only at quarterly meeting (P ~25%). Ask the champion to submit an agenda item; plan a 3-month timeline; use the wait for a pilot in another unit.
4. Forced RFP above threshold (P ~20%). Respond fully but keep effort small; if the scoring rubric favors large vendors, decline.
5. Bottom-up use by one department, system wants a contract (P ~15%). Build a system-wide business case; ask for an enterprise addendum with the BAA already in place.

**8. Common objections**
- "We need SOC 2 Type II." → "We're on Type I with Type II in progress; here's the bridge letter and pen test summary."
- "We need Epic integration." → "We support HL7 v2 and FHIR; we can start with a read-only interface in the pilot."
- "Your company is too small to be a critical vendor." → "Here's our business continuity plan and data export; we're happy to sign the escrow terms."
- "Put it in next fiscal year." → "What would it take to pilot in one unit this quarter using the existing budget?"

**9. Churn & seasonality**
- Annual logo churn: 8–12% for systems on contract; higher during EHR or leadership change [design].
- Renewal dynamics: changes in EHR, merger or acquisition, and CFO turnover drive churn. Multi-year contracts reduce it.
- Busy: spring (budget decisions) and September–October (fiscal planning for July 1 start). Slow: late November to early January, and July (new fiscal year freeze).

**10. Solo-founder fit: 0.2 for systems; 0.5 for critical access and community hospitals.** Success needs a compliance and security function you won't have; target small hospitals that accept a BAA and questionnaire without a full VAC cycle, or sell through a reseller or partner.

## Segment 3: Pharma and life sciences companies (GxP validation, compliance, long cycles)

**1. Size & examples**
- Typical: biotech 20–500 employees (pre-commercial); mid-size pharma 1,000–10,000; large pharma 10,000–100,000+. CROs and CDMOs in between.
- Examples: a 60-person clinical-stage biotech running trials through a CRO; a CDMO with 3 GMP sites; a mid-size generics maker; a top-20 pharma's R&D quality group.
- Budget: software spend per function often sits in R&D IT, Quality, or Clinical Ops. Startups selling to pharma most often sell into Quality (QMS, training, document control) or Clinical Ops.

**2. Buying committee** (counts [design])

| Role | Typical titles | Count | Influence | Main concern | Can block? | Presence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| User | QA specialist, validation specialist, clinical ops manager, lab tech | 3–10 | 0.4 | Usability, audit trail, workload | Yes, through rejection | Daily |
| Champion | QA manager, head of clinical operations, director of quality systems | 1–2 | 0.8 | Audit readiness | Yes | Weekly |
| Economic buyer | VP Quality, VP Clinical Ops, CIO for enterprise | 1 | 0.9 | Budget, risk | Yes | Monthly |
| Validation / CSV lead | CSV specialist, computer systems validation lead | 1–2 | 0.8 | Part 11, CSA evidence, change control | Yes, hard veto | Gate |
| IT / cybersecurity | IT security, cloud architect | 1–3 | 0.7 | SOC 2, access, data residency | Yes | Gate |
| Supplier quality | supplier qualification lead, vendor audit team | 1–2 | 0.8 | Vendor audit, quality agreement | Yes, hard veto | Gate |
| Legal / privacy | contracts, privacy (GDPR for EU trials) | 1–2 | 0.6 | Quality agreement, liability | Yes | Late |
| Procurement | category manager, strategic sourcing | 1 | 0.6 | Price, terms | Yes | Late |
| Regulatory affairs | regulatory lead | 0–1 | 0.5 | Impact on filings | Yes, rarely | Occasional |

- Buyer is not the user in nearly all deals; the validation lead is the most powerful non-signer.
- Quality agreements, supplier audits and validation evidence are standard parts of the committee's work.

**3. Cycle & deal size** (vendor and agency sources, [unverified]; design estimates otherwise)
- Agency page: 120–365 days for pharma and biopharma, $60k–$800k ACV ([devcommx, undated, unverified](https://www.devcommx.com/ai-sdr/durham/pharma)). Biopharma SaaS model: 6–18 months ([Compound VC, undated](https://businessmodels.compound.vc/model/saas-for-biopharma)). Clinical-trial tech interview: 18–24 months (unverified transcript).
- <$5k: department card; 2–6 weeks, but usually a non-GxP tool.
- $5–25k: 2–4 months; validation package required.
- $25–100k: 6–9 months; supplier audit and quality agreement.
- >$100k: 9–18 months; enterprise IT and validation.
- Typical small-vendor ACV: $15k–$60k for a single site; $60k+ for multi-site [design].
- Win rate: 15–25% on qualified leads [design].

**4. Procurement**
- Card: none for GxP-relevant systems; use P-card only for non-GxP tools.
- Quotes: 3 bids above $25k at large pharma [design].
- Board approval: capex or opex committee above $100k [design].
- Fiscal year: calendar year most common; budgets set Q3–Q4.
- Payment terms: net 30–60; POs required.
- Paperwork, all typically required:
  - Supplier qualification questionnaire (often 100–300 questions) and remote or on-site supplier audit.
  - Quality agreement with right-to-audit and change notification clause.
  - Validation package: URS-to-test traceability, release notes with change impact, audit trail evidence, Part 11 controls (21 CFR 11.10) for regulated records.
  - SOC 2 Type II, pen test, data residency statement.
  - W-9, insurance COI (E&O and cyber $2M–$5M) [design], DPA or GDPR terms for EU trial data.
  - VPAT is rarely required; HIPAA BAA only where patient data is involved (rare in pharma).
- Timeline effect: qualification must finish before purchase ([ISPE-style guidance via IntuitionLabs, unverified](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/gxp-saas-release-validation-index)). Expect 2–6 months for supplier qualification alone for a startup [design].
- FDA CSA: the final "Computer Software Assurance for Production and Quality System Software" guidance covers cloud and SaaS tools in quality systems and uses a risk-based approach. I could not retrieve the FDA page to confirm its issue date (a secondary source gives Feb 3, 2026), so verify on fda.gov before citing.

**5. Channels**
- Works: ISPE and PDA conferences and local chapters; peer referral from a QA or CSV lead at another company; a validated reference customer; partner with a validation consultancy (they recommend tools); LinkedIn outreach to QA directors (works, despite low reply rates).
- Fails: cold email to VPs; free trials in a regulated environment (no validation, can't go live); ads; general "AI" pitches (quality people are skeptical).
- Best times: mid-week, avoid the week before quarter-end and the holiday window.
- Tech-savviness: medium-high in clinical ops and IT; high skepticism everywhere.

**6. Methodology**
- MEDDPICC with a quality gate. Add the "P" (paper process) to the list: the supplier-qualification process is the paper process. Lead with the validation package and audit readiness.
- Challenger works for quality leaders who have seen a bad inspection; the message is "your last audit finding is a risk you can close with this."

**7. Scenarios**
1. The CSV lead rejects the product on evidence grounds (P ~35%). Send a validation pack and a 90-minute walkthrough of the audit trail; agree on a remediation plan.
2. Supplier audit scheduled for month 4 (P ~40%). Prepare an audit-ready folder now; offer a remote audit; name a quality contact.
3. Champion moves to another company (P ~20%). Re-run stakeholder map; the new owner often re-qualifies vendors.
4. Procurement pushes a multi-year contract with a termination-for-convenience clause (P ~15%). Price the paid pilot; accept annual terms with a multi-year discount.
5. Release change breaks validation (P ~15%). Publish release notes with change impact; offer a revalidation plan. Product-level change control is a selling point.

**8. Common objections**
- "We need a validated system before we can use it." → "Here's our validation package; we can run a 30-day IQ/OQ on your instance."
- "Your company isn't big enough to audit." → "Here's our SOC 2, pen test and an on-site or remote audit slot."
- "We don't have budget until next year." → "Would a validated pilot on one study fit this year's budget?"
- "Can you guarantee Part 11 compliance?" → "The regulated company remains accountable; we provide the controls and evidence, and we'll walk your CSV team through them."

**9. Churn & seasonality**
- Annual churn: 5–10% once validated, since switching costs revalidation. Churn spikes after a failed audit or a company acquisition.
- Renewals: tied to annual validation review and audit cycles.
- Busy: Q1 and Q3 (audit planning and budget). Slow: late December, August.

**10. Solo-founder fit: 0.2.** Validation evidence, supplier audits and quality agreements need a QA function a 1–3 person company does not have. Only a narrow product with a validation partner or reseller is realistic.
