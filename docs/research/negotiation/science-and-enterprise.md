# Negotiation science and enterprise SaaS deal evidence (2025-2026)

Scope: facts for the LLM counterparty and the scoring of the player's negotiating. Tags: **[sourced]** = a URL was retrieved in this pass; **[unverified]** = recalled from literature or seen only in secondary/vendor material; **[design]** = our assumption, not a statistic.

Source quality warning: most 2025-2026 SaaS procurement numbers come from vendor marketing (Vendr, Tropic, Spendflo, Redress Compliance, GrowthSpree). Treat them as directional. Where we could not retrieve a primary report, the figure is flagged.

## 1. Negotiation science

**Anchoring and first offers.**
- The original study is Galinsky & Mussweiler (2001), *JPSP* 81:657-669, which found the first offer predicts final settlement and that the first mover in either role did better. [sourced: https://www.london.edu/faculty-and-research/academic-research/f/first-offers-as-anchors-the-role-of-perspective-taking]
- A 2025 meta-analysis of first offers ("The power and peril of first offers...", *OBHDP*) was reported in a secondary aggregator. We could not confirm its pooled effect size. [unverified]
- Precise anchors pull harder than round ones: Janiszewski & Uy (2008), *Psychological Science* 19(2):121-127, found people adjust less from precise anchors. Later negotiation work reports precise offers reduce counteroffers even when the recipient knows the precision is a tactic. [sourced: https://www.scinapse.io/papers/2049235312 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision_bias]
- Design implication [design]: a first offer at a precise number (e.g. $47,500 not $50,000) should move the counterparty's counter more than a round one. Score it, but cap the bonus.
- Caveat: an anchor that is too aggressive can cost the relationship. The "winning the battle, losing the war" paper on anchoring in distributive deals is the relevant reference. [sourced: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/F38433715E3D9D74BF5ACFCE6869BE83/S1930297500006410a.pdf]

**Concessions.** Standard finding: concessions shrink over rounds, and a large early concession signals a soft position. Reciprocity is strong: a concession invites one back. [unverified: general literature; not retrieved in this pass]

**Integrative vs distributive.**
- Warsitzka et al. (2024, *Journal of Applied Psychology*) quasi-meta-analysis: 38,063 negotiations for joint gains and 21,271 for impasse rates. Number of issues had no effect on joint gains up to three issues and a negative effect beyond three. No issue-count effect on impasse rate. [sourced via search summary only; no DOI retrieved, verify before citing in-game]
- Interests-vs-values experiment: joint outcomes were higher when negotiators discussed interests, and the gain was largest under low time pressure. [sourced via search summary; verify]
- Design implication [design]: the game should have 3-5 tradeable issues (price, term, payment, SLA, liability). Bundles with more than 3 issues should show the player fewer obvious trades, not more.
- Value left on the table: we could not verify a specific "mythical fixed pie" study percentage. Do not put a number on this in the game without a source. [unverified]

**Deadlines and time pressure.**
- Don Moore's deadline papers (JESP 2004; OBHDP) report that final deadlines reduce stalling and that negotiators who reveal deadlines did better. [sourced via abstracts: https://search.isc.ac/Inventory/10/148160.htm ; https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/negotiation-skills-daily/get-time-on-your-side/]
- A 2019 *Experimental Economics* replication (Kocher et al.) found last-minute agreements rise under deadlines, but the effect disappears once disagreements are counted. [sourced: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/F388EC46629F094DDB48A96AE00F8DCC/S138641571901130Xa.pdf]
- Design implication [design]: a deadline raises agreement rate near the end but lowers joint value. Score both, so "close at the last minute" is not automatically a win.

**Reservation price, BATNA and ZOPA.** These are textbook concepts (Raiffa; Fisher & Ury). We did not retrieve a new source. [unverified: standard framing]. The key game rule: a player who reveals their walk-away price loses value; a counterparty who knows it can extract the surplus.

**Impasse.** A 2022 *Journal of Management* review argues that negotiators may prefer impasse over agreement even with a positive ZOPA. We did not find a reliable single impasse-rate figure. Warsitzka et al. (above) can be used for impasse-rate patterns. [sourced: review abstract; no percentage]. Do not invent a base-rate; use [design] values and label them.

**Walking away.** Credible walk-away threats improve outcomes; bluffs are detected over repeated rounds. [unverified: standard literature]

**Information asymmetry.** Each side's private information (true budget, alternatives) drives most of the surplus. Over repeated interaction, counterparties adjust their model of the player's BATNA. [unverified: standard literature]

**Preparation.** Better-prepared negotiators get better outcomes in most studies; the effect is largest on how often they make the first, precise offer. [unverified]

**Gender and culture.** Effects are real but inconsistent across studies. Keep out of the scoring logic. [unverified]

## 2. Enterprise SaaS procurement 2025-2026

**Discount benchmarks (conflicting, use ranges):**
- Vendr's own pages cite an average saving of about 11% across 40,000+ deals, and a Q1-Q2 2023 trend of discounts falling from 11% (2022) to 7% (Q2 2023). [sourced: https://www.vendr.com/insights/saas-trends-report-2023-q2 ; https://vendr.com/marketplace/captivateiq]
- Vendr vendor pages show larger per-vendor averages, for example 21.7% on renewals and 9.4% on new purchases for CaptivateIQ, and 20.2% for Glean. These are selection-biased (renewal savings include reduced seats). [sourced: vendr.com marketplace pages]
- Qualified: 22.9% average savings against a $51K median contract value. [sourced: https://vendr.com/marketplace/qualified]
- Monetizely 2025: 23% off list for 3-year commitments. Methodology unverified. [sourced: https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/saas-pricing-benchmark-study-2025-key-insights-from-100-companies-analyzed]
- GrowthSpree (marketing agency): median 12% off list; $200K+ enterprise deals average 22%; end-of-quarter discounts 60-110% deeper than mid-quarter. Unverified methodology. [sourced: https://www.growthspreeofficial.com/blogs/b2b-saas-discount-rate-benchmarks-2026-deal-discount-by-acv-stage-end-of-quarter-impact]
- Working range for the game [design]: 5-10% for sub-$10K deals, 10-20% for $25-100K, 15-25% for $100K+ with a multi-year term. Do not present as market fact.

**Vendor give-get trades (typical, vendor-specific):**
- Multi-year: about 5% for a multi-year term, plus about 5% for early renewal (WorkRamp example). [sourced: https://vendr.com/buyer-guides/workramp]
- Case study or logo: about 5% (Clockwise example). [sourced: https://vendr.com/buyer-guides/clockwise]
- Seat/tier milestones and volume thresholds (Zendesk: discounts often start around 50 users, community comment). [sourced: https://vendr.com/buyer-guides/zendesk; anecdotal]

**Procurement tactics and timing.**
- End-of-quarter pressure: sellers discount more near quarter close. Tropic's procurement leader describes the cycle that erodes pricing credibility. [sourced qualitatively: https://street-pricing-with-marcos-rivera.castos.com/episodes/stop-creating-the-boogeyman-how-saas-sellers-undermine-their-own-pricing-michael-shields-tropic]
- A "Vendr 2025 benchmark" figure says procurement extracts 11.4% on deals over $50K, falling to 4.2% when AEs prepare before procurement joins. Could not confirm against Vendr's publication. [unverified: from a sales-training page]
- Competitor quotes, "budget is X", and asking for 20-40% discounts are common opening moves [unverified: practitioner norm].
- Microsoft removed volume discounts from enterprise agreements starting 1 November 2025, which pushes buyers toward other levers. [sourced: https://www.vertice.one/blog/5-key-software-trends-for-procurement-and-finance-leaders-q4-2025]

**Renewal price caps.**
- Common cap language: 3-5% per year or CPI, whichever is lower. Advice-level only; not a measured market norm. [sourced as advice: https://www.softwareseni.com/how-to-negotiate-better-saas-contracts-and-lock-in-lower-prices]
- Observed uplifts: 8-12% annually typical, 15-25% for aggressive vendors (one secondary source). 79% of IT leaders reported a renewal price increase in the past 12 months (Zylo 2026 index). [sourced secondhand: https://www.spendhound.com/blog/saas-ai-renewal-negotiation-tactics-whats-working ; https://zylo.com/blog/better-buyer-saas-pricing]
- About a third of vendors include uncapped renewal uplift language (one secondary source). [unverified]

**Liability and data terms.**
- Vendor-side default: liability cap equal to fees paid in the previous 12 months. Mid-market caps often 100-200% of annual fees. [sourced: https://www.cloudnuro.ai/blog/saas-liability-cap ; https://www.jonathanlea.net/blog/liability-caps-technology-contracts/]
- Common landing zone: direct damages at 1x fees; data breach, confidentiality and gross negligence at a 3x super-cap rather than unlimited. [sourced: https://www.cloudnuro.ai/blog/saas-liability-cap]
- Worked example from the same source: a $900K breach against a 12-month cap ($120K) recovers $120K; a $2M data super-cap recovers in full.
- MFN (most-favored-nation) pricing clauses: no usable source found. [unverified; treat as a term the game can include with a [design] label]
- Payment terms net-60/90, auto-renewal, termination for convenience, SLA credits, indemnities, and the security addendum: standard buyer asks; no benchmark retrieved. [unverified]

**Who negotiates.** Procurement runs the process, legal owns the redline, the champion pushes internally, and finance signs off on the budget. We did not retrieve a primary source for redline cycle length. [unverified; the GrowthSpree claim that security and contract phases add 4-8 weeks is marketing copy]

**When vendors cave too much.** Deep discounts set renewal anchors: the next renewal is negotiated from the discounted number. Precedent spreads across accounts when the same concession is known. [unverified: practitioner norm; the Tropic discussion above supports the qualitative point]

## 3. Vendor-side purchasing for a founder (cloud/SaaS)

- AWS EDP discounts: one consultancy gives about 5-8% (3-year) and 7-10% (5-year) for $1-3M annual commit; another gives 10-18% (1-year) and 15-22% (3-year). Inconsistent; AWS does not publish its table. [sourced: https://redresscompliance.com/aws-edp-discount-benchmarks.html]. Not relevant at startup scale; ignore for the early game.
- Commit-vs-usage: committing to spend you will use beats a deeper discount on spend you won't. [sourced: https://redresscompliance.com/finops-data-edp-negotiation-leverage.html]
- Startup credits: AWS Activate up to $100K+ (general pricing guide). Current eligibility not verified. [unverified: https://costbench.com/software/cloud-infrastructure/aws/discounts/]
- Practical founder asks [design]: annual prepay for 10-20% off, startup-program credits, and a price-lock letter when signing a multi-year deal.

## 4. M&A and acquisition offers for small SaaS

- Holdback / indemnity escrow: about 5-20% of price held 12-24 months (advisor range); a 5-15% range with 12-18 months is also common. A 10%+ holdback is described as common; 90% of 2023 deals had some escrow. A true escrow is held by a neutral agent; a holdback stays with the buyer. [sourced secondhand: https://livmo.com/blog/escrow-holdback-ma/ ; https://ecosistemastartup.com/?p=62540]
- Earnouts: in 22% of private M&A deals in 2024 (SRS Acquiom 2025 study, secondhand). In SaaS, often 20-40% of price. Average payout reported at 21 cents on the dollar; median period 24 months (White & Case 2025, secondhand). [sourced secondhand: https://livmo.com/blog/earn-out-saas-acquisition-seller-guide/]. Treat payout figures as indicative.
- Earnout metric: net revenue retention is a stronger metric than gross revenue because it requires the buyer to keep customers. [sourced secondhand]
- LOI: sets exclusivity, price, cash/stock mix, escrow language. Exclusivity length (commonly 30-90 days) not verified. [unverified]
- Retrade risk: buyers reprice after diligence. Mitigate by pinning survival periods and liability caps in the LOI, since LOIs often leave them open. [sourced: https://www.l40.com/insights/letter-intent-m-a]

## Points for the game

1. First offers and precision: reward precise, reasoned first offers; penalize early huge concessions. [design]
2. Deadlines: score both agreement and joint value; disclosed deadlines reduce stalling. [design]
3. Issue count: keep 3-5 live issues per round.
4. Enterprise discount ranges: use the [design] bands above; expose vendor-side averages only as labeled references.
5. Caps and escrow: use 1x direct / 3x data super-cap and 10-20% holdbacks, labeled as typical.
6. Every unverified figure needs a [design] or [unverified] tag in the game text.
