### Lease terms (general, mostly non-US sources)

- Typical term: 3-5 years for a conventional lease. [design; not verified for 2025 US market]
- Security deposit: one or two months' rent is typical. Some landlords ask for a full year when they are hesitant or want a personal guarantee. Negotiate interest on the deposit and a step-down as payments stay on time. Source: https://businesslink.ca/dealing-with-personal-guarantees-and-deposits-for-commercial-tenants/
- Personal guarantee: negotiable for founders with a solid balance sheet. Alternatives are a larger cash deposit, a letter of credit (fees, may need collateral) or a parent guarantee. Check whether the guarantee is capped or burns down over the term. Source: https://www.higgsllp.co.uk/articles/should-you-personally-guarantee-a-commercial-lease
- Landlords commonly ask for 2-3 years of personal tax returns or a personal financial statement from a young or weak company.
- Build-out and tenant-improvement allowances, clawbacks and sublease rights were not found in 2025-specific sources. A local commercial real-estate lawyer should review them. [design]

## 2. Trade-offs with evidence

### Productivity and retention

- **Bloom, Han and Liang, Nature 630, 920-925 (2024).** Randomized trial with 1,612 Trip.com graduate employees over six months. Hybrid (3 days in office, 2 remote) vs fully onsite. Quit rates fell about a third in the hybrid group. No measurable effect on performance reviews, promotion rates or lines of code. Trip.com estimated the attrition savings at millions of dollars. Sources: https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2024/06/hybrid-work-is-a-win-win-win-for-companies-workers and https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/06/240612113235.htm. Caveat: a single large firm in China, with a managerial and engineering mix.
- **Stanford WFH Research (Bloom, 2025 talk).** Share of full US paid days worked from home is roughly 25%, flat since 2023 and about 5x the pre-pandemic level. Office swipe-card data show the same flattening. Managers and professionals typically come in 2-3 days a week. Source: https://www.mmi.mit.edu/_files/ugd/29d096_92e89762e7a44721a91544a7e2f2a033.pdf (2025 slides; the live series is at wfhresearch.com, not re-checked).
- **Gallup.** Hybrid workers spend about 46% of their week in the office (about 2.3 days), up from 42% in 2022 with no change after 2023. Gallup's tracker, as summarized secondhand, shows 52% hybrid, 26% fully remote and 22% fully on-site for US employees (May 2026) [unverified: secondhand]. https://www.gallup.com/workplace/694361/hybridworkretreatbarely.aspx

### Hiring pool and remote postings

- **Indeed Hiring Lab.** Remote postings were 7.7% of US postings at end-April 2025, down from 7.9% at end-March. Peak was 10.4% in 2022. Latest figure found is May 2025. https://www.hiringlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/May-US-Chartbook.pdf
- **LinkedIn applications (via Fortune, Jan 2024, citing LinkedIn).** Remote jobs were about 10% of openings and drew about 46% of applications, roughly 5x the competition per posting. LinkedIn 2022 data: 16% of openings, 44% of applications. https://dc.fortune.com/2024/01/29/remote-jobs-scarce-many-applications-labor-market-linkedin-report/
- A WhatJobs page claims remote and hybrid roles get 75% of applications. Not traced to a primary source; do not use. [unverified]
- Jobgether (Dec 2025) claims remote roles get 3-6x the applications of on-site ones. Single job board. [unverified]

Design read: a remote posting gets roughly 3-5x the applicants, so the applicant pool is large but thin. Any salary or quality effect is not established in these sources. [design]

### In-person founder norms

- YC's 2026 batch is described (aggregator pages plus YC's own about page) as in person in San Francisco, with a 3-day in-person kickoff, weekly SF meetups, and partner group office hours every two weeks plus one-on-one office hours. Remote accommodation was offered in 2022. Whether founders must relocate in 2026 was not confirmed. https://ycombinator.com/about [partly unverified]

### Offsites

- Multi-day offsite: about $1,000-3,000 per person is a common planning range (vendor blogs). https://retreatsandvenues.com/blog/company-retreat-budgets
- Conference and offsite cost in Europe: €201/person/day in 2025, of which €75 day rate and €126 per night accommodation (MICE Report 2026, via Naboo). https://www.naboo.app/en-us/blog/corporate-offsite-cost-2026
- Small companies (10-50 people): $500-1,000 per person, per a single vendor [unverified]. Vendor figures skew high; use your own quotes.

### Home-office stipends

- SHRM (2025 benefits survey, secondary summaries): roughly 53-56% of employers subsidize remote equipment or home office. Average among those that offer it is about $891-901/yr. Not an average paid to every remote worker. Verify against the SHRM report. [unverified]
- Compt 2024 benchmark: average home office stipend $507/yr. Benepass 2025 median WFH fund: $720. [unverified]
- Owl Labs: only 34% of workers reported any home-office stipend. Employer surveys report higher coverage than worker surveys.

## 3. Startup specifics

### WeWork as a cautionary tale

- Filed Chapter 11 on 6 Nov 2023. Lease liabilities were about two-thirds of operating costs, and it moved to reject leases at mostly closed sites. Landlords were the main losers, and bankruptcy law caps their lease damages. https://commercialobserver.com/2023/11/wework-bankruptcy
- Members were told memberships would not change, and operations continued. Membership fell from about 700,000 at end-2022 (Bisnow) to about 550,000 (Deskmag, undated).
- Emerged in June 2024 after eliminating over $4bn in debt. Company projected full profitability after interest and taxes in 2025 (projection, not verified). https://www.deskmag.com/en/coworking-news/what-does-the-new-wework-look-like-or-does-wework-still-exist

Lesson for the game: a flexible membership is cheap insurance against a dead landlord or operator. A 5-year lease with a personal guarantee is the opposite.

### When startups get an office [design]

- Solo or pair, fully remote: no office. Use a coworking membership or home.
- 3-8 people: a flexible desk or hot-desk plan in a coworking space, at about $200-600 per desk per month, scaling with use.
- 8-20 people or client-facing needs: a dedicated-desk or small-private-office coworking contract, or a short conventional lease.
- 20+ with a fixed team footprint: a conventional lease only after cash runway covers rent through the term.

### Common mistakes

- Signing a 5-year lease before the team exists. Rent is a fixed cost for the full term.
- Personal guarantees on a lease the company cannot yet carry.
- Overbuilding the fit-out (build-out is a sunk cost; it does not come back on downsizing).
- Downsizing into a lease that cannot be sublet. Subleases are usually at a discount and need landlord consent.
- Choosing a coworking plan on sticker price while ignoring contract length, meeting-room fees and the operator's health.
- Assuming remote hiring is free: the applicant pool is larger but more competitive per role.

### Founder isolation and productivity

- The sources in this slice do not give quantified solo-founder productivity effects. The Trip.com and WFH Research results above are for employees, not founders. [unverified for founders]
- Hybrid evidence suggests a 2-3 day office rhythm is the common choice for managers and professionals, and lowers attrition. [design application to founder teams]
