# Brief: research on churn reasons by customer segment, for a realistic SaaS founder simulator (late 2026)

Customers who buy software from a small vendor churn for reasons that differ a lot by segment. A roofer
goes out of business or the season ends. A school district loses its funding when ESSER ends. A
hospital's champion leaves. A DTC brand switches to Shopify's built-in feature. Research WHY customers
in your assigned segments cancel or don't renew SaaS, and how often each reason occurs.

Use web search a lot. Prefer 2023–2026 sources: Recurly, Paddle/ProfitWell, ChartMogul, Baremetrics,
ChurnBuster, Churnkey, RevenueCat, Gainsight, Vitally, Totango, vertical SaaS investor letters and S-1s
(e.g. ServiceTitan, Toast, Procore, Clio, Mindbody, Jobber, Shopify apps), SBA/BLS business-survival
data, founder posts on Reddit, Indie Hackers and HN, and G2/Capterra "switched from" reviews. Cite a URL and
date for every number. Mark your own estimates [design], and mark claims from secondary or vendor sources [unverified].

For EACH assigned segment, write a section with these headings:
1. Churn level: annual logo churn and revenue churn for SaaS sold to this segment (monthly vs annual
   contracts); involuntary churn (failed payments) share.
2. Reason mix: a table of 6–12 cancellation reasons with an estimated share (%) for each, summing to
   about 100%. Use ids from this list where they fit, and add segment-specific ones:
   business_closed, seasonal_pause, budget_cut, funding_ended, price_too_high, not_using / low_adoption,
   champion_left, acquired_or_merged, switched_competitor, platform_built_in (e.g. Shopify/Microsoft
   bundling), missing_feature, poor_support, bugs_reliability, onboarding_failed / never_activated,
   outgrew_product, consolidated_vendors, security_or_compliance, contract_term_end_rfp (forced rebid),
   moved_to_in_house, payment_failed (involuntary), owner_retired_or_sold, regulation_change.
3. Leading indicators: what signals precede churn in this segment (usage drop, a seat decline, a
   champion's LinkedIn job change, missed invoices, support tickets, budget season, new leadership).
4. Timing: when churn happens (renewal date, fiscal-year end, seasonality, first 90 days), and how much
   notice the vendor gets.
5. Save tactics that work, and how often they work: pause plans, downgrades, discounts, executive
   sponsor outreach, QBRs, multi-year deals, integrations that create switching cost. Give save rates if known.
6. What a solo founder can realistically do.
7. Sources: a list of URLs.

Write ONLY your output file (path given in your task). Don't edit anything else, don't commit, and don't
kill processes. Keep it dense: 400–900 words per segment.
