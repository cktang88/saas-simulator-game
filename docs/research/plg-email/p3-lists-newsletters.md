# P3: Owned audience, mailing lists and email marketing (research, as of 2026-10-15)

Conventions: numbers carry a source tag. [design] = our estimate, not a statistic. [unverified] = secondary or vendor claim we could not trace to primary data. "undated" = the source gave no date. Vendor benchmarks are measured on the vendor's own customers and are not comparable across tools.

Cross-cutting caveats
- Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates opens. Weight click-to-open and clicks, not raw open rate.
- Kit (formerly ConvertKit) and Mailchimp have no current primary creator benchmark that we could retrieve. The Kit page we found is a 2022 report (https://convertkit.com/email-marketing-stats, dated 2022). Use it only as a dated anchor.
- Waitlist-to-paid and lead-magnet numbers come almost entirely from marketing blogs. Treat them as ranges, not targets.

---

## 1. Pre-launch waitlists and launch lists

**Fits:** B2C, prosumer and PLG SMB products with a self-serve signup. Weak fit for enterprise, where design partners and pilots are the real list.

**Setup and weekly work:** One landing page with a one-field email form, a referral or position boost, a clear problem statement and a "what do you use today" question. Send 2–3 pre-launch updates. Personally invite the first 50 people in the list. Roughly 10–15 hours to set up and 3–5 hours a week during pre-launch [design]. Charging a deposit or offering founding-member pricing is the cheapest real demand test.

**Outcome ranges:**
- Landing-page median is 6.6% across about 41,000 pages (Unbounce, Q4 2024), with 11.4% at the 75th percentile, reported by ClickMinded (https://www.clickminded.com/landing-page-conversion-benchmark/, undated page citing Q4 2024 data).
- Waitlist-to-paid: a Medium guide cites 2–5% typical and 10–15% top performers [unverified] (https://medium.com/@soyzamudio/maximizing-waitlist-conversion-rates-data-driven-strategies-6856cb91f83b, undated). A calculator tool labels 15–25% as top decile [unverified] (https://www.flowjam.com/blog/waitlist-conversion-calculator, 2026). Sources disagree on what "conversion" means.
- Design estimate [design]: median 3–5% of waitlist emails pay within 30 days of launch; top quartile 10–15% when a deposit or founding price was used.

**Stellar vs average:**
- Stellar: segments by the pain they signed up for, asks a use-case question, converts the top referrers personally, runs a deposit or founding-price test, and launches to a cohort in waves.
- Average: counts signups as demand, leaves the list silent for months, and sends one launch blast.
- Mediocre: buys traffic to inflate the list, so the list is large and cold.

**Mistakes and consequences:**
- A long gap between waitlist and launch decays interest. Re-engagement drops and launch emails meet a cold list [design].
- Counting signups as validated demand. Founders then over-build for people who never pay.
- Cold-list launch blasts draw spam complaints (see lever 4 for the Google 0.10% and 0.30% thresholds).

**Sources:** ClickMinded (above); Medium (above); FlowJam (above); Unbounce Q4 2024 as relayed by ClickMinded.

## 2. Founder newsletters (Kit, beehiiv, Substack)

**Fits:** B2C and SMB founders building an audience before or alongside a product. Works for building in public and for niche B2B. Enterprise founders get less from it directly.

**Setup and weekly work:** Choose one platform, one cadence and one promise. A weekly issue costs a solo founder about 4–8 hours including research, writing and the send [design]. Content types that work for SaaS founders: build-in-public updates with real numbers, teardown and "how we did X" posts, a changelog-style section, and short curated links. Keep the paid tier separate and late.

**Outcome ranges:**
- Open rates. Omnisend's 2025 cross-industry data (27,000+ brands): 30.41% open, 2.44% click-to-open, 0.74% click-through, 0.20% unsubscribe (https://www.omnisend.com/blog/email-marketing-benchmarks/, 2025). beehiiv's self-reported 2025 average open rate of 41.24% comes via a secondary roundup (https://voxbooster.com/blog/newsletter-statistics-2026, 2026) [unverified]. Kit's 2022 figure of about 36% for January 2022 is dated (https://convertkit.com/email-marketing-stats).
- Paid conversion (free to paid): small and mid lists 1–3%, 2–5% at thousands of subscribers, and roughly 3% average. A 2026 guide reports a median of only 0.62% across newsletters (https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/newsletter-statistics-2026-data-points, July 2026) [unverified]. Specialized publications convert 4–10% and general-audience ones 1–2% [unverified].
- List growth rate: we found no reliable benchmark. Anecdotes range widely. Treat any growth claim without a timeframe and a denominator as marketing.
- Design estimate [design]: a consistent weekly newsletter with a clear promise reaches 1,000 subscribers in 3–9 months through owned channels alone, and 10,000 is a multi-year outcome without a distribution engine.

**Stellar vs average:**
- Stellar: same day and time every week, one named reader persona, measures click-to-open and replies, prunes at 90–180 days of inactivity, and reports growth monthly.
- Average: sends irregularly, optimizes for opens, and treats the list size as the product.
- Mediocre: abandons after 6–8 issues, or pitches the product in every issue.

**Mistakes and consequences:**
- Inconsistent sending. Subscribers forget you and unsubscribes and complaints rise after long gaps [design].
- Reading Apple-inflated opens as engagement. Sponsors then get inflated numbers and lose trust once the real click rate shows.
- Paywalling too early. Free-to-paid stays near the low end of the ranges above and the free list stops growing.

**Sources:** Omnisend (above); VoxBooster (above); DigitalApplied (above); Substack/Kit/beehiiv numbers as cited above.

## 3. Lead magnets, content upgrades, gated content, webinars, email courses

**Fits:** B2B SMB and PLG products where a specific asset (template, calculator, teardown) matches a buyer problem. Webinars fit mid-market and enterprise better than B2C.

**Setup and weekly work:** A lead magnet takes 8–20 hours to produce (template, calculator, checklist) [design]. A webinar takes 10–15 hours per event including promotion [design]. Email courses are mostly automation: 5–7 emails, each ending with the product's relevant step. Build once, maintain monthly.

**Outcome ranges:**
- Landing-page median 6.6%, top quartile 11.4% (Unbounce Q4 2024 via ClickMinded, see lever 1).
- Lead magnet pages: typical 5–20%, cold traffic 3–10%, warm traffic 15–30% with a strong offer (Leadpages, undated, https://www.leadpages.com/blog/landing-page-conversion-benchmarks-2026) [unverified].
- Form friction: a one-field popup converted at 4.41% versus 1.83% with three fields, across 329M impressions (Sleeknote via Komo, undated) [unverified].
- Specificity: generic newsletter asks 0.5–1.5%, a specific asset 5–15% [unverified] (Unilink, https://www.unilink.us/blog/how-to-build-email-list-2026, 2026).
- Webinars: ON24 reports registration-to-attendance averaging 60% in 2025 data (ON24 blog, 2026) [definition includes on-demand]. Goldcast's 2024 data gives 33% attendance (https://www.goldcast.io/blog-post/2025-webinar-statistics-b2b-marketers-should-know, 2025). The gap is definitional.
- Email courses: no benchmark found. Design estimate [design]: 15–30% of enrollees finish a 5-day course, and 1–3% of enrollees buy within 30 days.

**Stellar vs average:**
- Stellar: the magnet solves one job the product also solves, so every subscriber is a real prospect. Checks the magnet's downstream conversion, not just the opt-in rate, and cuts magnets that bring non-buyers.
- Average: generic "ultimate guide" PDFs that attract students and competitors.
- Mediocre: gates content behind forms that readers resent.

**Mistakes and consequences:**
- A magnet that attracts the wrong audience fills the list with people who never buy, which lowers engagement and deliverability for the paying segment [design].
- Wasted production hours on assets nobody converts from.
- Webinars with no follow-up sequence: attendees go cold within a week [design].

**Sources:** ClickMinded; Leadpages; Komo (Sleeknote); Unilink; ON24 blog (2026); Goldcast (2025).

## 4. Double vs single opt-in, consent, list hygiene, sunsetting, purchased lists

**Fits:** Every list. This is a legal and deliverability lever first.

**Opt-in data:**
- Double opt-in vs single: GetResponse's 2022 SMB study (2.76 billion newsletters, premium accounts, https://www.getresponse.com/blog/new-research-epic-battle-double-single-opt, 2023 post) reports subscription rate 1.28% single vs 0.33% double. Open rate 35.72% double vs 27.36% single, click-through 4.19% double vs 2.36% single, unsubscribe 0.1084% double vs 0.0913% single. Conversion to paying customers was not reported. GetResponse is a vendor.
- Mailchimp's study of 30,000 accounts, as relayed by List Health (https://www.mailblaze.com/thinking/double-opt-in-vs-single-opt-in, undated), reports double opt-in lists with 72.2% more unique opens, 114% more clicks, and 48.3% fewer bounces. The original was not linked and the study did not control for niche [unverified].
- Design [design]: double opt-in for purchased or cold-sourced lists, and for any list where deliverability matters. Single opt-in is acceptable for in-product signups with a clear consent statement.

**Legal, with exact text:**
- US CAN-SPAM (FTC guide, https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business, fetched 2026-10-15): opt-outs must be honored "within 10 business days." Each violating email can be penalized up to $53,088. Every message needs a valid physical postal address. You may not sell or transfer an opted-out address. The law covers B2B email.
- Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules (Google, https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126, fetched 2026-10-15): senders over 5,000 messages a day to Gmail need SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Marketing messages must support one-click unsubscribe and show a visible unsubscribe link. Google says to keep spam rate "below 0.30%" and to monitor it at "below 0.10%," avoiding "0.30% or higher." Google's page gives no deadline for honoring unsubscribes. Vendor pages say two business days [unverified].
- Canada CASL (statute S.C. 2010, c. 23): express consent is the default. Implied consent from a purchase or contract lasts two years [secondary, undated]. Maximum administrative penalties reported as C$10 million per violation for businesses, and directors and officers can be personally liable [secondary]. Pre-checked boxes do not count as express consent.
- UK PECR soft opt-in: applies only where the contact came from a sale or negotiation, the marketing covers similar products, and an opt-out was offered at collection and in every email. It does not cover new or prospective contacts. The ICO guidance itself was not retrieved, so verify before relying on it [secondary].
- EU GDPR: consent or a documented legitimate interest is needed, and inactivity profiling can need consent [secondary]. Verify with counsel.

**Hygiene and sunsetting:**
- Industry practice (secondary): sunset after 90–180 days without opens, run a re-engagement sequence first, then suppress rather than delete [unverified].
- Unsubscribe benchmarks: 0.1–0.5% per send is typical (MailMunch, undated); 0.20% in Omnisend's 2025 data.

**Purchased lists:** Do not do it. [design] Cold lists generate spam traps, complaints and bounces, which drive domain reputation down. In CASL and EU jurisdictions it is unlawful without consent. Under CAN-SPAM the sender still bears liability for each email. ESP terms typically ban it, and the account can be suspended with the whole list.

**Stellar vs average:**
- Stellar: explicit consent with a stated purpose, a preference center, an automated sunset flow, suppression of opt-outs across tools, and a written consent record per subscriber.
- Average: a checkbox added after the fact, no sunset, and suppression done by hand.
- Mediocre: buys a list "to test" and ships it.

**Consequences (traceable):**
- Gmail spam rate above 0.30%: delivery moves to spam, then deferrals or rejection (secondary description of Google's guidance). Recovery takes weeks [design].
- CAN-SPAM: up to $53,088 per violating email, in theory.
- CASL and PECR: penalties and, for CASL, a private right of action [secondary].
- Account suspension at the ESP, with the list lost [design].

**Sources:** FTC CAN-SPAM guide (fetched); Google sender requirements (fetched); GetResponse 2023 study (fetched); List Health / Mailchimp (secondary); Zoho / Suped on Google thresholds (secondary); CASL and PECR secondary summaries; MailMunch and Omnisend (above).

## 5. Newsletter growth tactics: referrals, recommendation networks, cross-promotion, paid per subscriber

**Fits:** Newsletter and list businesses. For SaaS, the same mechanics apply to a product-referral loop, covered in the referrals playbook.

**Setup and weekly work:**
- Referral program: 4–6 hours to set up on a platform that supports it [design]. beehiiv's referral program sits on its Scale plan at $39 a month (Ruzuku guide, undated) [unverified].
- Recommendation networks and marketplaces: 1–2 hours a week to choose and schedule swaps [design].
- Paid acquisition: needs budgets and tests (see below).

**Outcome ranges:**
- beehiiv's own case study: The Brink added 14,000 subscribers in its first month using built-in referral tools (beehiiv blog, undated) [vendor].
- beehiiv's Workspaces case study credits Top 4 Recommendations with 67% of new subscribers in one month (beehiiv blog, undated) [vendor].
- A single creator's review says referrals brought about 160 subscribers a month, or 57% of growth, over eight months (Sidestackers, April 2026) [unverified, single anecdote].
- Join rates for referral programs run 5–30% (Prefinery, undated). Viral coefficient above 1.0 is the aim, but a k of 0.5 or more is strong and k above 1.0 is rare outside consumer social apps (Track360, undated). Consumer internet: 0.15–0.25 is good, 0.4 great, 0.7 outstanding (Viral Loops, undated) [vendor].
- Recommendation marketplaces: about $0.50–$5 per subscriber, depending on overlap and quality [unverified].
- Paid social per subscriber: one agency reports $0.80 per subscriber at scale [vendor claim]. PostHog's handbook reports Reddit signups at $50–$90 each, about 20 times worse than Meta, and LinkedIn clicks above $5, roughly 10–20 times Meta's. It also says it stopped paid newsletter placements because cost per conversion was too high (PostHog handbook, undated).
- Design estimate [design]: a paid-subscriber CAC of $1–$5 on broad social is realistic for a consumer-interest newsletter. For a B2B audience it is $10–$50, and most of those subscribers won't be buyers.

**Stellar vs average:**
- Stellar: reward thresholds (for example, 3 and 10 referrals), a referral-quality check (open after 14 days), and paid tests with a cost-per-paid-subscriber target rather than a cost-per-signup target.
- Average: referral rewards with no quality check, and swaps with mismatched audiences.
- Mediocre: buys cheap lists or blasts sponsorship inventory it cannot deliver.

**Mistakes and consequences:**
- Fake or gamed referrals. Subscribers who never open lower the sender's engagement and deliverability for the real list [design].
- Mismatched cross-promotions. Unsubscribes rise and the sponsor's brand is hurt.
- Paid subscribers who never open: the list grows while the CPM you can charge falls (lever 7).

**Sources:** beehiiv blog (undated); Ruzuku (undated); Sidestackers (April 2026); Prefinery, Track360, Viral Loops (undated); GrowSurf case study (a Brazilian newsletter reached 339,000+ subscribers through referrals, undated); PostHog handbook (undated).

## 6. Converting subscribers to customers: launch emails, nurture, segmentation

**Fits:** Any product with a list. B2C and PLG are the strongest fit. Enterprise uses nurture to feed sales.

**Setup and weekly work:**
- Build a 5–7 email onboarding or nurture flow: 6–10 hours [design]. Maintain 1–2 hours a week.
- Launch sequence (design): a teaser, a launch-day email, a "last call" email, and a founder note to the top engagers. Expect most conversions in the first 48 hours of each send.
- Segmentation by interest: tag on click and on in-product behavior. Start with 3–5 segments, not 20 [design].

**Outcome ranges:**
- PostHog reports onboarding flow open rates of 52–56%, click-through 4–7.4%, and conversion 3–6%, with conversion defined as enabling billing within a week (PostHog blog, undated) [single company].
- Activation-based benchmarks: over 40% acceptable, over 55% great, over 70% strong (Sequenzy SaaS benchmarks, undated) [vendor]. Trial expiry emails open 40–60% [vendor].
- Free-to-paid newsletter conversion (lever 2): 1–3% for small lists, around 3% average, median 0.62% [unverified].
- Design estimate [design]: onboarding and nurture conversion of 3–8% of new subscribers to paid within 30 days for a consumer product with a clear trial. For a B2B product with a card-required trial, a low single-digit percentage is typical.

**Stellar vs average:**
- Stellar: each email maps to an activation step, the sequence exits when the user converts, and conversion is measured per segment.
- Average: a generic weekly newsletter with a discount link.
- Mediocre: blasts a discount to the whole list at every launch.

**Mistakes and consequences:**
- Launch blasts to unengaged subscribers. Complaints and inactive-list penalties follow (lever 4).
- Discount-only conditioning. Revenue arrives but churns, and the list learns to wait for discounts [design].
- No segmentation: interested buyers and tire-kickers get the same email, and unsubscribes concentrate on the wrong group.

**Sources:** PostHog blog (undated); Sequenzy (undated); DigitalApplied (July 2026).

## 7. Newsletter monetization as a side business

**Fits:** Newsletters with a specific audience, mostly B2B or niche consumer. Sponsorship income matters only above a few thousand engaged subscribers.

**Setup and weekly work:** Sales and placement take 3–5 hours per placement, less through marketplaces [design]. Set a fill target, typically one sponsor per 2–4 issues, and avoid selling a slot to a sponsor whose product the list would hate [design].

**Outcome ranges (beehiiv's sponsorship guide, fetched 2026-10-15, https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/newsletter-sponsorship-cost, undated):**
- CPM general: $10–$75 per 1,000 subscribers. Broad consumer $15–$35. B2B specialized $50–$100+. Under 5,000 subscribers $15–$35 CPM.
- Flat rate per placement: under 5,000 subscribers $50–$250. 5,000–50,000 $500–$3,000, with most at $1,000–$1,500. 50,000–100,000 $3,000–$7,000. Over 50,000 $3,000–$20,000+.
- To justify the rate: a 40%+ open rate and 3–5% click-through on sponsored links (beehiiv's stated benchmark, undated) [vendor].
- Paved's Q3 2026 benchmarks, as relayed by a secondary article, give a median sponsorship CPM of $32 per 1,000 subscribers, and HR at $18.47 against lifestyle at $1.22 per 1,000 [unverified]. The $1.22 figure is inconsistent with beehiiv's $10 floor, so the basis probably differs. Do not model from it.

**Worked example [design]:** 5,000 engaged subscribers at a $20 CPM is $100 per placement. At one placement every two issues, that is about $200 a month, before platform fees. Sponsorship alone rarely covers the founder's hours at this size.

**Stellar vs average:**
- Stellar: sells on click-through and reply data, discloses sponsorships clearly, and says no to misfit sponsors.
- Average: sells on list size, and reports open rate without clicks.
- Mediocre: overlaps sponsors, inflates numbers, or sends undisclosed ads.

**Mistakes and consequences:**
- Undisclosed sponsorships breach FTC endorsement rules (16 CFR Part 465; not verified in this session). Consequence: legal exposure and reader trust loss [design].
- Irrelevant sponsors lift unsubscribes, which lowers the list's value to the next sponsor [design].
- Overselling slots. Sponsors then see low click-through and churn.

**Sources:** beehiiv sponsorship guide (fetched; undated); Paved summary (secondary, 2026); PostHog handbook (undated).

## 8. Broadcast strategy: frequency, product-update and changelog emails

**Fits:** Newsletters, and SaaS with a paid base that needs product news.

**Setup and weekly work:** A monthly changelog email takes 1–2 hours [design]. Product-update emails to paid users can run from a segment, not the whole list.

**Frequency evidence (weak and contradictory):**
- One vendor blog claims unsubscribes peak at once or twice a month, and daily sends are no worse than weekly. The claim is attributed to an individual with no data shown [unverified] (https://emaillistvalidation.com/blog/unsubscribe-rate-by-send-frequency-how-often-is-too-often/, undated).
- Another source reports low-frequency lists lose more subscribers and cites about 0.5% for weekly lists with no source [unverified] (https://www.mailpro.com/email-campaign/email-marketing-statistics/unsubscription-rate.asp, undated).
- A Count.co example reports a tech newsletter's unsubscribe rate hitting 3% after moving from weekly to three times a week, and recovering after reverting (https://count.co/metric/unsubscribe-rate, undated). It is illustrative, not controlled.
- No rigorous study was found. Design rule [design]: change frequency only in a segmented test, watch unsubscribes and Gmail spam rate for four weeks, and revert if either rises.

**Unsubscribe and open benchmarks:**
- Unsubscribe: 0.20% (Omnisend 2025); 0.1–0.5% typical (MailMunch, undated); about 0.1% (Webmecanik, undated).
- SaaS open rates conflict: 28.1% (Hostinger, undated) vs 18.4% (secondary, undated) [unverified]. Mailchimp's 2024 marketing average was 21.33% (secondary) [unverified].

**Stellar vs average:**
- Stellar: separates transactional and marketing messages, sends changelog to paid users in-product plus by email, and cuts subscribers who never open after the sunset flow.
- Average: sends every product update to the full list.
- Mediocre: announces every minor release as a "big launch."

**Mistakes and consequences:**
- Frequency spikes raise unsubscribes and complaints. The Gmail 0.30% threshold is the hard limit (lever 4) [design].
- Product-update blasts to unengaged subscribers damage reputation even when the content is useful [design].

**Sources:** EmailListValidation, MailPro, Count.co (undated); Omnisend; MailMunch; Webmecanik; Hostinger; ClickMinded (undated).

## 9. SMS and push as adjacent owned channels (brief)

**SMS:**
- US marketing texts require prior express written consent under the TCPA, plus carrier brand and campaign registration (10DLC), per secondary summaries [unverified].
- Klaviyo's 2026 benchmarks, as relayed by a secondary article: good campaign click rate 8.9–14.5%, unsubscribe 0.6–1.4%, flow click rates near 10% (Klaviyo benchmarks, 2026) [unverified]. Sakari reports SMS opt-outs usually below 3.5% per send and CTRs of 18–35%, with mixed definitions [unverified].
- Design [design]: SMS works for B2C products with a transactional reason to text (order updates, trial expiry). It is poor for B2B SaaS. Consequence of consent failure: TCPA statutory damages per message (the statute provides $500 per violation, up to $1,500 when willful; not verified in this session).

**Push:** Not researched in depth. Treat it as a mobile-app-only lever with opt-in rates that are low in practice [design, unverified].

**Stellar vs average:** Stellar sends a text only when there's a transactional reason and a clear opt-in. Average blasts promotions. Mediocre buys numbers.

**Sources:** Klaviyo benchmarks (2026, via secondary); Sakari (undated); ClickMinded SMS benchmarks (2026); statute references are from memory, not fetched.
